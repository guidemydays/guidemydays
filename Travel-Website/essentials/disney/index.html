Best of Disney Parks

Everything here is about when.

Everything on this page is about when: the best weeks to book, which morning is worth being at the gate for, when each booking window opens and in which timezone, and which hours of the day are worth spending on a queue. Walt Disney World in Orlando and Disneyland Resort in Anaheim run on genuinely different clocks — the same trip plan applied to both will waste a day. Prices and hours move constantly; the windows and rules below are current for 2026.

1Choosing your dates
When to go
Walt Disney World

The quiet weeks in Orlando

  • Late August through September, skipping Labor Day weekend, is the lightest stretch of the year across all four parks. January and early February are the cool-weather equivalent. The two other gaps are early November outside Veterans Day weekend, and the first ten days of December after Thanksgiving but before the holiday rush.
  • The hidden cost of a quiet week is short hours. A 9am–7pm September day gives you far less usable park time than a 9am–11pm July day, so a low-crowd trip needs more days, not fewer. Check the posted hours for your dates before deciding how many park days to buy.
When to go
Disneyland

The quiet weeks in Anaheim

  • Mid-January to mid-March and mid-September to mid-November are the shoulder seasons. Within any week, Tuesday and Wednesday are consistently the two quietest days and weekends are consistently the worst — at a resort this heavily driven by local annual passholders, the day of the week matters more than it does in Orlando.
  • Summer is peak, with one counterintuitive exception: the week of 4 July often runs lighter than the weeks around it, because annual-pass blockouts and top-tier single-day ticket pricing both bite hardest on those dates. D23, the Disney fan convention, is at Anaheim 14–16 August 2026 and fills both the parks and the surrounding hotels.
When to goIn the park
Walt Disney World

Building the day around Florida weather

  • June to September is the wet season, and the storms are a schedule, not a risk: near-daily thunderstorms roll through in the mid-afternoon, most often between 2pm and 5pm. Outdoor coasters and anything with an exposed queue shut down for lightning and can stay down for 30–45 minutes after the last strike.
  • Plan the exposed park for the morning and put an indoor block in the afternoon — a show, a table-service meal, or the hotel pool. Coming back for the evening after the storm clears is the standard summer shape of the day and the reason evening park hours run so late.
When to go
Both resorts

Hard-ticket party nights close the park early

  • On Mickey's Not-So-Scary Halloween Party nights, Magic Kingdom closes at 6pm to anyone without a party ticket. Party guests can enter from 4pm and the event runs 7pm to midnight. The 2026 season starts 7 August, with dates on Tuesdays, Fridays and Sundays in August and September, and most nights of the week through October. Mickey's Very Merry Christmas Party works the same way in November and December.
  • This catches people who bought a regular ticket for a party date and lose the evening. Check the park calendar for your dates before you buy, and if you land on a party night, either buy the party ticket or make that a different park's day. Disneyland runs the same model with Oogie Boogie Bash at California Adventure in the autumn.
2Sixty days out — dining
Book ahead
Walt Disney World

The dining window opens 60 days ahead at 6am

  • Reservations open 60 days before the dining date at 6:00am Eastern. Guests staying at a Disney resort hotel, Shades of Green, the Swan and Dolphin or Swan Reserve get the better version: 60 days before check-in you can book the whole stay in one sitting, up to 10 days of reservations. Everyone else books one day at a time, returning at each individual 60-day mark.
  • Be logged in and on the reservation screen before 6:00am, not at 6:00am — the hardest tables go in the first minutes. Space 220, Cinderella's Royal Table, Be Our Guest, 'Ohana and Victoria & Albert's are the ones that vanish fastest.
Book ahead
Disneyland

The dining window opens 60 days ahead at 6am

  • Same 60 days, same 6:00am — but Pacific time, and one day at a time. There is no length-of-stay privilege for hotel guests here, so a five-night trip means five separate booking mornings. Blue Bayou, Oga's Cantina, Napa Rose and Carthay Circle are the ones that need the alarm.
  • If Blue Bayou is gone for dinner, try lunch — same room, slightly shorter menu, and noticeably more cancellations.
Book ahead
Both resorts

The cancellation clock is the second chance

  • Both resorts charge a per-person no-show fee if you cancel inside two hours of the reservation, which means inventory reliably drops back into the app right around that two-hour line. Check the morning of your visit and again about two hours before the slot you want — those are the two moments when tables reappear.
  • The mobile walk-up list is separate from reservations and opens at park opening for participating restaurants. Join it early in the day even if you have no intention of eating until evening; the position is what you are queuing for.
3Seven days out — Lightning Lane
Lightning Lane
Walt Disney World

Lightning Lane Multi Pass — the booking window

  • Guests at a Disney resort hotel, Shades of Green, the Swan and Dolphin or Swan Reserve can buy and book from 7 days before check-in, covering the whole stay up to 14 days. Everyone else books from 3 days before their first park day. Either way the window opens at 7:00am Eastern on your first eligible morning.
  • That four-day gap is the single largest practical difference between staying on property and off. On a busy week the on-site window is often the difference between a booked morning slot for a headliner and a mid-afternoon one.
Lightning Lane
Walt Disney World

Tiers, and the three-at-a-time rule

  • You hold three selections at once. Magic Kingdom, EPCOT and Hollywood Studios split the list into two tiers: of your three pre-booked picks, at most one can be Tier 1. Animal Kingdom has no tiers at all, so all three picks are free choices there.
  • Once you tap into one of the three, the slot frees up and you can book a fourth, then a fifth, all day. The pre-booked three are the floor, not the ceiling — people who never re-book after tapping in get a third of the value they paid for.
Lightning Lane
Walt Disney World

Single Pass rides are bought separately

  • Five attractions sit outside Multi Pass and are sold one at a time: Seven Dwarfs Mine Train and TRON Lightcycle / Run at Magic Kingdom, Guardians of the Galaxy: Cosmic Rewind at EPCOT, Star Wars: Rise of the Resistance at Hollywood Studios, and Avatar Flight of Passage at Animal Kingdom.
  • They open on the same 7-day and 3-day schedule as Multi Pass, and the good return times on the top two or three go within the first hour of the window. If a Single Pass ride is the reason someone in your party wanted this park, buy it the moment the window opens rather than deciding at the gate.
Lightning Lane
Both resorts

Premier Pass buys the whole park for a day

  • Premier Pass is one-time entry to every Lightning Lane attraction in a park for a day, with no bookings to make and no return windows to manage. At Walt Disney World it is priced per park per date, running from roughly $119 at Animal Kingdom to around $449 at Magic Kingdom. At Disneyland it spans both parks and includes the two Single Pass headliners.
  • Numbers sold are capped and it frequently sells out for busy dates. It is worth the money only on a single-day trip where every hour has to count — over a week it costs more than the extra park days would.
Lightning Lane
Disneyland

Disneyland's Lightning Lane runs on a different clock

  • You can buy Multi Pass up to 7 days ahead from 7:00am Pacific, or on the day — but buying is not booking. The first selection can only be made once you have physically tapped into a park, so there is no pre-dawn booking race the way there is in Orlando.
  • After that you hold one selection at a time, not three, and there are no tiers. The next booking unlocks when you redeem the current one or two hours after you booked it, whichever comes first — so a selection with a distant return time still lets you book again after two hours. Setting a two-hour phone timer is the whole strategy.
  • One purchase covers both Disneyland and California Adventure, so there is no per-park decision to make. Prices start around $30 and move with demand; the exact price for a given date posts about 21 days ahead. Star Wars: Rise of the Resistance and Radiator Springs Racers are the two Single Pass rides.
4The morning — rope drop
Rope drop
Both resorts

How early you actually need to be there

  • Aim to be at the tapstiles 45 to 60 minutes before the posted opening, and 60 to 75 on a peak date. Both resorts routinely start scanning people in ahead of the published time, so the crowd that arrives "on time" is already behind the crowd that is inside.
  • Work backwards from the tapstile, not from the park: parking, tram, security and, in Orlando, an internal transport leg all sit between the car and the gate. The first 90 minutes after opening are worth roughly three hours of the same day at 2pm, which is why the arithmetic is worth doing.
Rope drop
Walt Disney World

Early Theme Park Entry — 30 minutes, every park, every day

  • Guests of any Disney resort hotel, plus the Swan and Dolphin, Swan Reserve and Shades of Green, get in 30 minutes before official opening at all four parks, every day. Disney has confirmed the benefit through 2027. Only a subset of attractions operates during the half hour, so check what is running before choosing which park to use it on.
  • It is genuinely the cheapest advantage on the property, and it shifts the arrival maths: on-site guests should target 75 minutes before the posted opening, which is 45 before their own.
Rope drop
Disneyland

Early entry no longer exists in Anaheim

  • Disneyland's 30-minute early entry for hotel guests ended on 5 January 2026. It has been replaced by one complimentary Lightning Lane Multi Pass entry per hotel stay — one per stay, not one per day — and it cannot be used on the two Single Pass headliners.
  • The practical effect is that everyone at Disneyland now starts at the same moment. Being physically early at the gate now matters more than which hotel you booked, and the old advice to pay for an on-site room purely for the morning head start no longer applies.
Rope dropIn the park
Both resorts

What to ride in the first hour

  • Go straight to the highest-demand ride that has no Lightning Lane booked, not the one nearest the entrance. The rides closest to the gate absorb the whole opening crowd; walking two minutes further routinely halves the first wait of the day.
  • Save anything you already hold a Lightning Lane for — spending the best hour of the day in a queue you have already paid to skip is the most common self-inflicted error at both resorts.
5Through the day
In the park
Both resorts

Park hopping — both time limits are gone

  • Walt Disney World dropped its 2pm hopping restriction in January 2024: once you have tapped into your first park you can move to any other at any time during its hours, subject to capacity. Disneyland's 11am rule ended on 9 June 2026 — Park Hopper tickets and every Magic Key tier can now cross at any time, though you still nominate a starting park when you make the reservation.
  • This changes what a hopper is for. It is no longer an afternoon add-on; it is a tool for taking two rope drops in one day at Disneyland, or for leaving a park the moment its evening show ends in Orlando.
Book ahead
Both resorts

Park reservations — required in one place, not the other

  • Walt Disney World dropped reservations for date-based tickets, the kind most visitors buy. Annual passholders still need one for most dates, though they can enter without a reservation on good-to-go days or after 2pm, except weekends at Magic Kingdom.
  • Disneyland still requires a park reservation for every ticket type, Magic Key included, with no announced end date. Book it the day you buy the ticket — popular dates run out of reservation inventory well before they run out of tickets.
In the park
Both resorts

The 11-to-3 trough

  • Waits peak between roughly 11am and 3pm at both resorts, and standby times in that window are typically double what the same ride posts at opening. Nothing you do inside the park changes that, so plan to spend it on something that is not a queue.
  • The good uses are the table-service meal you booked, an indoor show, or a trip back to the hotel. With Orlando resorts 20 to 40 minutes away this needs deciding in advance; at Disneyland the hotels are close enough to make a midday break realistic even on a single-day trip.
In the park
Both resorts

Parades and fireworks are a wait-time tool

  • Every scheduled show pulls a large share of the park into one place and holds it there. Waits on major rides fall noticeably during a parade and during the first of two fireworks performances — the standard trade is to watch the later show and ride during the earlier one.
  • The cost is the exit. A park emptying after fireworks takes 45 to 60 minutes to clear, and in Orlando that queue is for a bus or a monorail, not a footpath. Either leave 10 minutes before the finale or plan to stay another half hour.
In the park
Walt Disney World

Virtual queues, and when they come back

  • No attraction is on a virtual queue at Walt Disney World at the moment — everything runs standby plus Lightning Lane. The system now appears only for new ride openings, most recently Bluey's Wild World at Animal Kingdom, which moved to a standby line on 2 June 2026.
  • When it does return, the shape has been consistent: drops at 7:00am and 1:00pm, with the morning drop joinable from anywhere as long as you hold valid admission, and the afternoon one requiring you to already be inside the park. With new lands due at Magic Kingdom and Hollywood Studios, it is worth checking the app the night before during any opening week.
In the park
Walt Disney World

Extended Evening Hours are the best window of the week

  • Guests of Deluxe and Deluxe Villa resorts, plus the Swan and Dolphin, Swan Reserve and Shades of Green, can stay two hours past regular closing on select nights — usually Monday at EPCOT and Wednesday at Magic Kingdom. Confirmed through 2027.
  • With most of the park gone, waits in those two hours are the lowest of any time all week, early entry included. If your hotel qualifies, build the whole trip so that its park days land on those nights.
In the park
Both resorts

The last hour before close

  • Posted waits drop sharply in the final hour, and a queue you join before the posted closing time will be run to the end — the gate closes, the line does not. It is the second-best low-wait window of the day after rope drop and costs nothing.
  • Join the longest line you still want at about ten minutes before close. The posted time at that hour is usually inflated and you will often walk on.
6Getting around
Getting around
Walt Disney World

Allow a full hour, door to tapstile

  • Sixty minutes from a resort room to the park tapstile is the planning number, whichever transport you use. Buses, boats, the Skyliner and the monorail all queue at rope drop and again at close, which are exactly the two moments you care about.
  • The Skyliner does not run in lightning or high wind and is replaced by buses, slowly. On a summer afternoon, treat a Skyliner connection as something that may not be there when you need it.
Getting around
Walt Disney World

Magic Kingdom parking is not at Magic Kingdom

  • Driving to Magic Kingdom puts you at the Transportation and Ticket Center, across the lagoon. After the tram there is still a monorail or ferry leg, so add about 20 minutes on top of what the other three parks need. This is the single most common reason people miss their own rope drop.
  • The same leg exists on the way out and is worst immediately after fireworks. Staying 20 minutes past the crowd is faster than joining it.
Getting around
Disneyland

Two parks, one esplanade

  • Disneyland and California Adventure face each other across a single plaza — hopping between them is a five-minute walk, not a transport leg. That is the structural difference from Orlando, and it is why a Disneyland day can be planned as two half-days in different parks while a Walt Disney World day generally cannot.
  • Parking is the Mickey and Friends or Pixar Pals structure plus a tram. Allow 45 minutes from parking space to tapstile at opening; the tram queue at 8am is the bottleneck, not the drive.
Getting around
Both resorts

Arriving from the airport

  • Orlando International is 30 to 45 minutes from the Walt Disney World area. Disney's free Magical Express coach no longer exists, so the options are the paid Mears Connect shuttle, a rideshare, or a rental car — book the shuttle ahead, since it runs on a schedule rather than on demand.
  • For Disneyland, John Wayne in Santa Ana is about 20 minutes away and Los Angeles International is 45 to 90 depending entirely on the hour. If the flight lands into LAX between 3pm and 7pm, assume the long end.
7Tickets, passes & discounts
Tickets & passes
Walt Disney World

Multi-day tickets get cheaper by the day

  • A single-day, single-park ticket ranges from about $119 (Animal Kingdom, off-peak) to $209 (Magic Kingdom, peak dates) — 2026 is the first year a one-day ticket has crossed $200. The per-day price then drops fast: a 4-day ticket averages roughly $125–$140 a day, a 7-day ticket roughly $93–$95 a day.
  • Pricing is date-based and moves with demand, not a flat calendar — the gap between a value-date and an ultra-peak-date 5-day ticket can run over $600 per person. Buying two extra low-value days is routinely cheaper than a single peak-date day, which is the main argument for building the trip around the quiet weeks in Section 1.
Tickets & passes
Walt Disney World

Annual passes — four tiers, and two need Florida ID

  • The Incredi-Pass (roughly $1,600, no blockout dates) is the only tier open to anyone. Sorcerer (around $1,100), Pirate (around $870) and Pixie Dust (around $490) are sold only to Florida residents or Disney Vacation Club members, and all three carry blockout weeks around Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year — Sorcerer has the fewest.
  • An out-of-state family cannot buy their way past the Incredi-Pass price, whatever the trip length — if the maths only works at a lower tier, someone in the party needs Florida residency or DVC membership for it to apply.
Tickets & passes
Disneyland

Magic Key — four tiers, and the cheapest one is local-only

  • 2026 pricing runs from Imagine Key at $599 up to Inspire Key at $1,899, with Explore ($999) and Believe ($1,474) in between. Imagine is restricted to Southern California residents and is the most heavily blocked out — expect weekends, summer and most holidays to be closed to it.
  • Inspire and Believe both include unlimited digital PhotoPass downloads and a discount off Lightning Lane Multi Pass, on top of parking and merchandise/dining discounts that scale with the tier. The Enchant Key was retired on 13 January 2026 and replaced by Explore — if an older guide or forum post mentions Enchant, it is out of date.
Tickets & passes
Both resorts

Tickets are non-refundable, but a date-based ticket can move

  • Neither resort refunds a ticket once purchased. A date-based ticket can still be modified to a different unused date before the original date passes — if the new date costs more you pay the difference, and if it costs less there is no refund of that difference.
  • Buying through a third party (a travel agent, Costco, AAA) sometimes adds a cancellation or protection option Disney itself does not offer directly — worth asking about specifically if the trip dates are still soft when you buy.
Tickets & passes
Walt Disney World

Florida resident and military discounts are real, and separate

  • Florida residents get their own ticket and resort pricing — recent summer resort offers have run up to 30–35% off depending on stay length — and can access the lower three annual pass tiers above. These require ID at purchase and at the gate, and are not transferable to out-of-state guests in the same party.
  • Active and retired U.S. military (all branches, including Guard and Reserve) and their spouses qualify for separate discounted tickets, sold through Shades of Green or via ID.me verification — typically a meaningful discount off the equivalent public multi-day ticket. Check the "special offers" page for your dates before assuming the standard price is the only option.
8Where to stay
Book ahead
Walt Disney World

Four resort tiers, and what actually changes between them

  • Value (the three All-Star resorts, Pop Century, Art of Animation) starts around $150 a night off-peak and gets you a standard room with minimal theming. Moderate resorts (roughly double the Value rate) add real theming, larger rooms near 440 sq ft, and shower-tub bathrooms. Deluxe and Deluxe Villa run roughly $500–$700+ a night for walkable or boat-served park access and full-service dining.
  • Every tier — Value through Deluxe Villa — gets the same 30-minute Early Theme Park Entry and the same free Disney transport network; the tier buys you room quality, location and dining, not extra time in the parks.
Book ahead
Walt Disney World

Only Deluxe and Deluxe Villa unlock Extended Evening Hours

  • Value and Moderate resort guests get Early Theme Park Entry like everyone on property, but the two extra hours after closing described in Section 5 are reserved for Deluxe and Deluxe Villa guests (plus the Swan, Dolphin, Swan Reserve and Shades of Green). A Value-tier stay cannot access that window at any price.
  • If the lowest-crowd hours of the week are the priority, this is the single biggest functional gap between the tiers — bigger, in practice, than the difference in walking distance to the parks.
Book ahead
Disneyland

Only three hotels sit inside the resort

  • The Disneyland Hotel, Disney's Pixar Place Hotel and Disney's Grand Californian Hotel & Spa are the only properties physically inside the Disneyland Resort footprint. All three are walkable to both parks — there is no shuttle or monorail leg the way there often is at Walt Disney World.
  • Since Anaheim's 30-minute early entry for hotel guests ended on 5 January 2026 (Section 4), staying on-site here buys walking distance and a complimentary Lightning Lane entitlement per stay, not a head start on the clock.
Book ahead
Disneyland

Good Neighbor hotels are the budget option, not off-brand

  • Dozens of hotels around Anaheim are Disney-approved "Good Neighbor" partners — they can sell park tickets and vacation packages alongside your room, and most are a 10–20 minute walk or a short shuttle from the esplanade. They carry none of the three on-site hotels' perks (no complimentary Lightning Lane entitlement, no resort-exclusive events).
  • Because Disneyland's two parks share one entrance, the walk-distance gap between a Good Neighbor hotel and an on-site one matters less here than the equivalent gap at Walt Disney World, where an off-site stay usually means a car for every park visit.
9Traveling with kids & accessibility
Family & access
Both resorts

Rider Switch keeps a non-rider's party out of the queue twice

  • When someone in your party won't ride — too short, or unable to wait in the queue — ask the cast member at the attraction entrance for a Rider Switch. They scan the non-riding guests' tickets or bands and issue an entitlement in the app; the riding group then queues (standby or Lightning Lane) while the rest waits nearby.
  • Once the first group finishes, up to two of them can re-ride immediately with the person who sat it out, without rejoining the line. You need one entitlement in hand before you can request the next — they don't stack, so use it before asking for another.
Family & access
Walt Disney World

DAS registration opens 60 days out, by video chat

  • Disability Access Service can be set up 2 to 60 days before your first park day through a live video chat in the app or on the website, available 7:00am–8:00pm Eastern. The guest the pass is for must be present on camera; recording the call is not allowed, and a cast member takes a photo once approved.
  • Guests who skip advance registration can still do the same video call on the day of their visit, so arriving without one is not a dead end — it just means doing the call from inside the resort instead of from home.
Family & access
Walt Disney World

Baby Care Centers are free, and in every park

  • Each of the four theme parks has a dedicated Baby Care Center with nursing rooms, changing areas, and bottle-warming stations (sinks, microwaves, filtered water) — Magic Kingdom's is between Casey's Corner and Crystal Palace, EPCOT's is at the far end of the original pavilions near the Mexico side of World Showcase, Animal Kingdom's is beside Creature Comforts near the bridge to Africa. All are marked on the park map and free to use.
  • These double as the quietest air-conditioned break spot in a park if a toddler needs a reset that isn't a full hotel trip — worth locating on arrival even before you need it.
Family & access
Walt Disney World

Renting a stroller costs less by the length of stay

  • Disney's own single strollers run about $15 a day, or $13 a day if you prepay for the whole length of your stay; doubles run about $31 a day, or $27 for the length of stay. Rentals are first-come, first-served at all four parks and Disney Springs — there's no way to reserve one in advance.
  • Third-party companies (Kingdom Strollers and similar) deliver a stroller to your resort before you arrive and pick it back up at checkout, which avoids the daily line at the park entrance and is usually cheaper over a week-long stay — worth comparing before assuming the in-park counter is the only option.
10Tech, photos & merchandise
In the park
Walt Disney World

Mobile Order skips the quick-service line entirely

  • In the My Disney Experience app, order and pay ahead at any participating quick-service location, then pick a 30-minute arrival window that matches when you actually plan to eat. When you get there, tap "I'm here" in the app and your food is prepared to order — there is no separate line to join.
  • Order the moment you know roughly when you'll want to eat, not when you're already hungry — popular counters run out of near-term arrival windows well before the kitchen itself is busy, especially around the 11am–3pm trough from Section 5.
In the park
Walt Disney World

Merchandise gets to your room without you carrying it

  • Resort Delivery — telling the cashier to send an in-park purchase to your Disney resort room — is free and typically arrives by the next day. It only works if you're staying on Disney property; there is currently no in-park "package pickup" locker for same-day collection.
  • A package mailed or shipped to your resort's front desk carries a flat $6 delivery fee if you want Bell Services to bring it to your room rather than collecting it yourself. Shipping purchases straight home from the store is a separate paid service, priced per item at checkout.
In the park
Walt Disney World

Memory Maker — buy it before the trip, not during

  • Memory Maker (unlimited PhotoPass downloads for the length of your trip) runs about $185 bought in advance, $210 if bought during or after the trip, or $85 for a single day. It covers photographers stationed around the parks and select ride-photo and video moments, tied to your account via MagicBand, MagicMobile or Bluetooth on the app.
  • Photos expire 45 days after they're taken — not 45 days after the trip ends — so download or print anything you want to keep well before that clock runs out. Disneyland runs its own separate PhotoPass system with its own pricing; a Walt Disney World Memory Maker purchase does not carry over.
In the park
Both resorts

Street meet-and-greets need no reservation, just a line

  • Character meet-and-greets outside of dining rooms — Cinderella Castle, the Character Landing spots, Star Wars characters near Galaxy's Edge — are pure standby: join the posted queue, no advance booking exists for them the way it does for a table. The busiest ones behave like a ride, with the longest waits at midday and the shortest right after rope drop or in the last hour before close.
  • Character dining, by contrast, runs on the same 60-day reservation window as any other table (Section 2) — booking that window is the only reliable way to guarantee a specific character at a specific time, rather than hoping for a good queue.
