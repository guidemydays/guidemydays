Entry Requirements

eVisas and travel authorizations, before you board.

The pre-entry authorizations and eVisas a traveler has to arrange before boarding — ESTA-style travel authorizations, electronic visas and online arrival cards — with what each costs and how far ahead to apply. Eligibility for each system varies by passport, so confirm yours on the official site linked below. Countries that need a full visa application are on the Visas page, which answers for your own passport specifically. Rules here change with little notice, so the page also tracks the ones currently in flux, including ETIAS. It also covers what to expect at passport control once you land — typical wait times at several fleet-city airports, and the programs that speed the line up.

ETIAS has been delayed. In July 2026, EU-Lisa confirmed that a Q4 2026 launch was no longer feasible after technical issues with the EES border system. A revised timeline is not expected before 2027 at the earliest. Visa-exempt visitors will eventually need a quick online pre-authorization (€20, valid 3 years) to visit France, Italy, Spain, Germany, and 27 other Schengen countries — similar in shape to the US's own ESTA. No action needed until a launch date is confirmed.

Everywhere Else

Which countries are visa-free with no pre-travel step at all depends entirely on which passport you're holding — a list that is visa-free for one nationality can require a full visa for another. The Visas page answers this properly: pick your own passport there and see every destination in the matrix grouped by exactly what it needs, not a fixed example list. Standard rules still apply wherever no visa is needed: a passport valid at least 6 months beyond arrival, and no overstaying.

Entry rules change. Verify requirements with the destination's own immigration authority before every trip — especially for ETIAS, which has been delayed past 2026 with no confirmed new launch date.

Arrival Immigration Wait Times

How long passport control takes on landing depends far more on the airport than on any of the paperwork above — some of the busiest international arrivals halls among fleet-city airports run consistently long, others clear in minutes. Ranges below are typical, not guaranteed: a cluster of widebody arrivals or a single closed booth can push either figure higher. This is separate from Trusted Traveler Programs, which covers how to enroll in Global Entry or CLEAR — this section covers what to expect at the desk itself, enrolled or not.

Arrival Timing & Seasonal Peaks
  • Early morning arrivals — before the first bank of overnight long-haul flights lands — and late-evening arrivals after the daily rush both tend to clear fastest.
  • Landing within the same 30–60 minute window as two or more widebody long-haul flights is what actually drives a long line; check the arrival airport's daily schedule if timing is flexible.
  • Summer (June–August) and the December holidays add the longest waits system-wide — compounded at Schengen airports through 2026 by ongoing friction from the EES rollout.
  • Destination-specific holiday weeks (Thanksgiving week for US arrivals, Golden Week for Japan, Chinese New Year for East Asia) add extra volume on top of the usual daily pattern.
Desk Prep Checklist
  • Passport open to the photo page; boarding pass or paper arrival card ready if the destination still uses one.
  • Any pre-entry authorization confirmation (ETA, ESTA, eVisa — see above) saved offline, since arrivals-hall wifi isn't reliable everywhere.
  • The first night's accommodation address ready, in case an officer or arrival card asks for it.
  • Complete an e-gate or kiosk's prompts before joining the officer line where one is offered — a finished slip or scan skips a step at several of the airports above.
