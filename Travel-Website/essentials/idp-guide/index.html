International Driving Permit

A license translated into nine languages, and the countries that actually check for it.

An International Driving Permit is a certified translation of a valid driver's license, not a license of its own — it exists so a foreign officer or rental clerk can read what they're looking at. This page covers what it actually does, which destinations require one by law versus which ones just tend to ask for it at the rental counter, how to get one in the US through AAA, and what happens if you skip it where it's required. What an IDP Actually Is
Not a license
It's a certified translation of a valid driver's license into up to nine languages, issued under the 1949 Geneva Road Traffic Convention — so a foreign officer or rental clerk can read what they're looking at.
Only valid with the real thing
An IDP carries no photo-verified driving record of its own. It has to be shown together with the license it translates — never as a stand-alone document.
Good for one year
A US permit expires exactly a year after it's issued, tied to the license it translates — a renewed license needs a new permit, even mid-year.

Renting a Car Without One
A missing IDP can mean a denied rental, even with a confirmed reservation. Where the destination legally requires one, the counter is checking for it on pickup — a US license alone isn't enough. In a country where local law doesn't require it, many international rental chains still ask for one as their own house policy, so "not legally required" is no guarantee of an easy pickup. Driving without a required IDP also puts insurance coverage at risk if you're stopped or in an accident — it can be treated the same as driving without a valid license.
