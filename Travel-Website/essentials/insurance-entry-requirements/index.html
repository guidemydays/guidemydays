Travel Insurance

Required to enter, not just recommended.

The Schengen area requires proof of at least €30,000 in medical coverage from anyone applying for a visa, Cuba checks every visitor at the border regardless of nationality, and Ecuador and Turkey each tie a requirement to specific visa categories most travelers never expect. Below that legal layer sits a second, wider list — the United States, Switzerland and Japan — where nothing is checked on arrival but a single hospital bill can outrun an entire trip's budget. For coverage types, how to buy a policy, and credit-card benefits, see Travel Insurance.

Legally Required — Checked at the Border or the Visa Desk

In these four cases, proof of insurance is part of getting in or getting a visa — not just good advice. Coverage rules vary: some apply to every visitor regardless of nationality, others only to travelers who need a visa in the first place.

Visa applicants
Anyone applying for a Schengen visa must show travel medical insurance before the visa is issued — the requirement sits inside the visa application, not a border check. Visa-exempt nationalities (the US, UK, Canada and most others on short trips) are not checked for it on arrival, but the same coverage protects against the same costs either way.

Required — everyone
Cuba requires proof of medical insurance from every foreign visitor on arrival, regardless of nationality or trip length. Travelers who show up without it are sold a local policy on the spot — a few dollars a day — before they clear immigration. US-issued health insurance, including Medicare, is not accepted as proof.

EcuadorGalápagos entry, plus 3 visa types
Conditional
Mainland Ecuador dropped its general tourist-insurance requirement in 2022 — most visitors need none to enter. Two exceptions remain: everyone flying into the Galápagos Islands must show proof of valid travel health insurance regardless of visa type, since medical evacuation off the islands is expensive and limited, and three long-stay visa categories require insurance at the application stage, before the visa is granted.

TurkeyResidence permit applicants only
Residence permit
A tourist stay under 90 days needs no special insurance to enter Turkey — ordinary travel insurance covering medical treatment is enough. Anyone applying for or renewing a residence permit (ikamet) is a different case: private health insurance meeting a minimum coverage floor is a hard requirement, and the permit is not issued without it.

Not Required — But the Bill Can Wreck the Trip

Nothing below is checked at a border or a visa desk. All three are places where a single hospital visit can cost more than the entire trip, and where a non-local health plan typically pays nothing.

United StatesNo entry requirement
The US has no entry-level insurance requirement for any visitor. It also has some of the highest medical costs of any destination on this site — an emergency-room visit or a short hospital stay routinely runs into five figures for someone paying without insurance, and most non-US health plans provide no coverage at all once outside their home country.No legal requirement
SwitzerlandSchengen visa rule only
Switzerland carries no visitor-entry insurance mandate beyond the same Schengen visa-applicant rule that applies to all 29 Schengen countries (€30,000 minimum — see above). What makes it worth a separate note is cost: Swiss healthcare runs among the most expensive in Europe, and insurers commonly recommend limits of CHF 100,000 or more for a Swiss trip specifically, well past the Schengen minimum.No legal requirement beyond Schengen
JapanRule change starting April 2026
Japan currently has no insurance requirement for tourists. That is changing: starting April 2026, immigration will screen returning visitors for unpaid medical bills, and debt as small as ¥10,000 (about $64) can be grounds to bar re-entry. Mandatory insurance for all incoming tourists has been proposed and is expected to follow. Most non-Japanese health plans, including US Medicare and Medicaid, provide no coverage in Japan.No requirement yet — screening starts Apr. 2026
