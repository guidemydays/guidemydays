When to Buy Standalone vs. Use Your Card
Situation Recommendation Why
International trip with limited home-plan coverage
 Compare standalone Many cards have little or no emergency-medical coverage. Check your domestic plan first: Original Medicare usually excludes foreign care, but exceptions and supplemental coverage exist.
Pre-existing medical condition
 Buy standalone Some plans offer a pre-existing-condition exclusion waiver only when bought soon after the first trip payment and when other eligibility rules are met. Deadlines vary by plan.
Trip over $5,000 non-refundable
 Buy standalone Card limits and eligible purchases vary. A standalone policy may cover the insured, non-refundable trip cost when every required payment and deadline condition is met.
Adventure / remote destination
 Buy standalone Skiing, diving, trekking — many cards exclude "hazardous activities." Evacuation from a remote location can top $150,000.
Cruise
 Buy standalone Medical care on a ship is expensive; medevac to a port hospital is even more so. Cruise line insurance is often overpriced — compare independently.
Domestic trip in your own country, flexible bookings
 Card coverage likely enough Check network and out-of-area rules for your health plan. If bookings are refundable, cancellation exposure is lower; then compare the card’s delay and baggage terms.
Short domestic trip, under $1,500 total
 Skip or card only Insurance at 4–10% of trip cost isn't worth it for a cheap, mostly-refundable trip where your own health insurance is valid.

Key Things to Know
Buy early when you need time-sensitive benefits. Pre-existing-condition waivers and CFAR upgrades often have deadlines measured from the first trip payment, but the window and eligibility rules vary by plan.
Read what “covered reason” means. Standard cancellation reimburses only listed events and exclusions still apply. CFAR broadens cancellation reasons, usually reimburses only part of the insured cost, and has purchase and cancellation deadlines.
Payment rules differ by benefit. Some require the entire round trip on the card; others cover a trip when only part of the fare is charged or eligible card rewards are used. Read the exact guide before paying.
Check your existing health coverage first. Original Medicare usually does not cover care outside the US, with limited exceptions; some Medicare Advantage, Medigap, private, or national plans do provide foreign emergency benefits. A travel policy also has exclusions and never guarantees every cost.
Check the activity exclusions list. Skiing, diving, motorcycling, and high-altitude trekking may require a specific plan or upgrade. Coverage varies by activity, depth, altitude, licensing, and destination.
Compare cruise-line and third-party plans. Price, medical limits, supplier-default protection, cancellation reasons, and whether reimbursement is cash or future cruise credit can differ materially.
CFAR has a hard cancel deadline. Most Cancel For Any Reason policies require you to cancel at least 48 hours before departure — some require 72 hours. If you wait until the night before to cancel, CFAR won't cover you. Check your exact window when you buy.
