Six hoursis a document, not a delay.

A reference for six of the world's busiest connecting airports — what fits in a 4–8 hour transit window, the express train into the city when the clock allows it, where to nap or shower, where to store bags, and what a transit visa means for your specific passport. This assumes your layover is actually that long — minimum connection times are a separate question, answered on the Airport Connections page.

Asia

Singapore Changi

Singapore
SINHub

Consistently rated among the world's best airports for a layover — Jewel Changi's indoor waterfall and gardens sit right off Terminal 1, reachable without ever leaving the transit system.

Gate
Jewel
Tanah Merah
City
40–45 min by MRT
4–8hr plan
Jewel Changi (linked to T1, with bridges from T2/T3) for the 40m Rain Vortex waterfall, Canopy Park and a free light show most evenings — 1.5–4 hours covers it comfortably.
Express train
No dedicated express — the Changi Airport MRT branch connects via a transfer at Tanah Merah, roughly 40–45 minutes to the city center.
Transit hotel
Ambassador Transit Hotel (airside, Terminals 2 & 3, rooms by the hour) and Aerotel Singapore (airside, T1, with an outdoor pool). YOTELAIR sits inside Jewel but is past immigration.
Luggage storage
24-hour left-luggage counters in every terminal, including inside Jewel and at T2's MRT entrance.
Transit visa
Most transiting nationalities need no visa to stay airside. Singapore's 96-hour Visa Free Transit Facility separately lets a defined list of nationalities enter the city with a confirmed onward ticket — eligibility varies by passport.
Exit & re-entry
Staying inside the transit area skips immigration entirely. Leaving into the city means a normal entry and exit stamp, with immigration typically well under an hour each way.

Jewel Rain Vortex · 1.5–4 hrs

Tokyo — Narita & Haneda

Japan
NRT/ HND

Two airports serve Tokyo, and they're not interchangeable for a layover — Haneda sits close to the city center; Narita does not.

Gate
Monorail
N'EX
Tokyo Stn
HND 11–14 min · NRT ~53 min
4–8hr plan
Haneda: Edo Koji's Edo-period dining street and a free 24-hour observation deck (Terminal 3) suit a shorter layover, and its central location makes a quick trip into Tokyo realistic with 6+ hours. Narita: the free Narita Transit Program runs guided tours to Naritasan Shinshoji Temple for layovers of 8+ hours.
Express train
Haneda: Tokyo Monorail to Hamamatsucho (~13 min) or the Keikyu Line to Shinagawa (~11–14 min). Narita: the Narita Express (N'EX) runs direct to Tokyo Station in about 53 minutes.
Transit hotel
Haneda: the Royal Park Hotel's Transit wing (Terminal 3) sits genuinely airside on just an international boarding pass. Narita: the 9h capsule hotel (Terminal 2) is landside — using it means clearing Japanese immigration.
Luggage storage
Coin lockers throughout both airports (¥300–¥1,000/day) plus a staffed baggage counter at Narita Terminal 2.
Transit visa
Most nationalities can stay in the airside transit area without a visa at either airport. Actually leaving requires a Japan visa where one is normally needed, or a discretionary Shore Pass from the immigration officer — not guaranteed, and dependent on nationality.
Exit & re-entry
Leaving either airport means full immigration and customs both ways; reported waits run from a few minutes to well over an hour with little predictability — budget at least 3 hours round trip if you plan to exit.

HND observation deck, 24h

Seoul Incheon

South Korea
ICNHub

Incheon runs its own guided city-tour program for transit passengers, alongside a free Korean-culture museum for anyone staying airside instead.

Gate
K-Culture
AREX
Seoul Stn
43–51 min nonstop
4–8hr plan
Airside: the free K-Culture Museum and Korean Cultural Street (hanbok try-ons, craft workshops). With a valid visa/K-ETA, the airport's own free Transit Tour program runs guided loops from one hour up to six, covering palaces and traditional markets.
Express train
AREX (Airport Railroad Express) runs nonstop to Seoul Station in about 43 minutes from Terminal 1, 51 minutes from Terminal 2.
Transit hotel
The airside Transit Hotel in Terminal 2 (opposite Gate 251) is reserved for passengers connecting between international flights, sold in 6-hour blocks. The landside Darakhyu Capsule Hotel sits in both terminals' transportation centers.
Luggage storage
Staffed, insured counters in both terminals — T1 near Check-in Area N, T2 at Entrances 3 and 4.
Transit visa
South Korea exempts many transiting nationalities from a visa for stays up to 30 hours with a confirmed onward ticket; roughly two dozen nationalities are excluded. The Transit Tour program is a separate, tour-based route requiring registration. Verify by passport.
Exit & re-entry
Immigration runs anywhere from 15–20 minutes off-peak to 90+ minutes at peak (weekday afternoons, weekends, cherry blossom season). The Transit Tour requires formally entering Korea, so budget generously for both directions plus a security re-screening.
Best airside move
K-Culture Museum, free
To the city

Middle East

Dubai International

United Arab Emirates
DXBHub

Terminal 3's duty-free, lounges and a gym/pool/spa club fill a shorter layover without leaving the building; the Metro puts the city itself within reach on a longer one.

Gate
G-Force
Red Line
Burj Khalifa
23–27 min by Metro
4–8hr plan
In-terminal: duty-free shopping, the G-Force Club (gym, pool, spa) and Zen Gardens, all in Terminal 3. With 5+ hours and an eligible passport, the Metro reaches the Burj Khalifa/Dubai Mall area for a genuine city visit.
Express train
No separate express — the Dubai Metro Red Line runs directly from Terminals 1 and 3 to the Burj Khalifa/Dubai Mall area in about 23–27 minutes.
Transit hotel
Dubai International Hotel sits inside the Terminal 3 transit area itself (Concourses A/B/C), sold in 3-, 6-, 12- and 24-hour blocks without clearing immigration.
Luggage storage
24-hour left-luggage counters at both Terminal 1 and Terminal 3 arrivals.
Transit visa
50+ nationalities (UK, US, most of the EU, Australia, Canada and others) get visa-on-arrival and can walk straight into the city. Everyone else needs a pre-arranged 48- or 96-hour transit visa, and a handful of nationalities don't qualify for either — verify by passport.
Exit & re-entry
Leaving the transit area is a full UAE entry — your passport is stamped and the clock starts on your visa. Immigration averages about 12 minutes but can run to an hour at peak; allow 60–75 minutes for a same-terminal connection, ~90 for cross-terminal.

G-Force Club · gym, pool, spa

Europe

Amsterdam Schiphol

Netherlands
AMSHub

A small Rijksmuseum gallery and the Airport Library sit airside between the lounges — one of the few hub airports where a genuine city trip is realistic on a mid-length layover, transit-visa rules permitting.

Gate
Rijksmuseum
Schiphol Stn
Centraal
15–17 min, every ~10 min
4–8hr plan
Airside: the Rijksmuseum's airport gallery and the Airport Library near Lounges 2–3 for a quick, free stop. With 6–7+ hours and no Airport Transit Visa requirement, the train into central Amsterdam (canals, Dam Square) fits comfortably.
Express train
NS trains run directly from Schiphol Station, beneath the terminal, to Amsterdam Centraal every ~10 minutes — about 15–17 minutes each way.
Transit hotel
YOTELAIR Amsterdam Schiphol sits airside past security in Lounge 2, capsule-style, open only to transiting passengers. The landside Sheraton Amsterdam Airport connects directly but sits outside the secure zone.
Luggage storage
Coin lockers both pre- and post-security (Kelderplein and Holland Boulevard); a staffed 24-hour counter on Level -1 handles larger bags for up to 30 days.
Transit visa
As a Schengen airport, Amsterdam requires a Schengen Airport Transit Visa for a defined list of nationalities even to stay airside without entering the Netherlands — holders of a valid US, Canada or Japan visa are typically exempt. Verify by passport.
Exit & re-entry
Leaving the transit area means a full external Schengen border check each way. Schiphol's own guidance recommends a 2-hour buffer for a Schengen connection and 3 hours for a non-Schengen one.

Rijksmuseum gallery · free
To the city

Frankfurt

Germany
FRAHub

A free rooftop viewing terrace sits inside Terminal 2, and Frankfurt's own train station puts the old town within a single short ride.

Gate
T2 Terrace
ICE / S-Bahn
Römerberg
6–8 min ICE · 12–15 min S-Bahn
4–8hr plan
Airside: the Terminal 2 Visitor's Terrace, a free rooftop viewing deck. With 5+ hours and no Airport Transit Visa issue, the Römerberg old-town square is about 15 minutes away by train and easily walkable from there.
Express train
The airport's own long-distance station (ICE/IC) reaches Frankfurt Hauptbahnhof in about 6–8 minutes; the regional S-Bahn (S8/S9) takes roughly 12–15 minutes.
Transit hotel
MY CLOUD Transit Hotel sits airside in Terminal 1's non-Schengen transit area, booked by the hour. The landside Sheraton Frankfurt Airport connects to every terminal and both train stations via the SkyLine people-mover.
Luggage storage
Staffed counters at Terminal 1 Arrivals (24 hours) and Departures, plus Terminal 2 Hall D (6am–10pm).
Transit visa
The same Schengen Airport Transit Visa rules apply as Amsterdam — a defined list of nationalities needs one even to stay airside, with exemptions for holders of a valid US, Canada or Japan visa. Verify by passport.
Exit & re-entry
US citizens typically clear immigration in about 5 minutes via automated kiosks, other nationalities roughly 7–9 — longer at peak summer and holiday periods, and the EU's Entry-Exit System can add processing time on both exit and re-entry.
Best airside move
T2 Visitor's Terrace · free
To the city

Sleep airside
