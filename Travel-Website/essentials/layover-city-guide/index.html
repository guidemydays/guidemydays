Layover City Guide

A long layover isn't dead time — here's what six hours at six major hubs actually gets you.

A reference for six of the world's busiest connecting airports — what fits in a 4–8 hour transit window, the express train into the city when the clock allows it, where to nap or shower, where to store bags, and what a transit visa means for your specific passport. This assumes your layover is actually that long — see Airport Connections for minimum connection times.

Asia
Singapore ChangiSingapore · SIN
Guide
Consistently rated among the world's best airports for a layover — but Jewel Changi's indoor waterfall and gardens are landside, so a transit passenger must be eligible to enter Singapore and clear immigration.
4–8hr planWith at least five hours and permission to enter Singapore, clear immigration for Jewel Changi (linked to T1, with bridges from T2/T3), the 40m Rain Vortex, and Canopy Park. Staying airside, use the terminal gardens, free snooze lounges, and inter-terminal Skytrain instead.
Express trainNo dedicated express — the Changi Airport MRT branch connects via a transfer at Tanah Merah, roughly 40–45 minutes to the city center.
Transit hotelAmbassador Transit Hotel (airside, Terminals 2 & 3, rooms by the hour) and Aerotel Singapore (airside, T1, with an outdoor pool). YOTELAIR sits inside Jewel but is past immigration.
Luggage storage24-hour left-luggage counters in every terminal, including inside Jewel and at T2's MRT entrance.
Transit visaMost transiting nationalities need no visa to stay airside. Singapore's 96-hour Visa Free Transit Facility separately lets a defined list of nationalities enter the city with a confirmed onward ticket — eligibility varies by passport.
Exit & re-entryStaying inside the transit area skips immigration entirely. Jewel is in the public area: visiting it or the city requires clearing arrival immigration, entering Singapore, and completing departure immigration and security again.
Tokyo — Narita & HanedaJapan · NRT / HND
Guide
Two airports serve Tokyo, and they're not interchangeable for a layover — Haneda sits close to the city center; Narita does not.
4–8hr planHaneda: Edo Koji's Edo-period dining street and a free 24-hour observation deck (Terminal 3) suit a shorter layover, and its central location makes a quick trip into Tokyo realistic with 6+ hours. Narita's relaunched Transit & Stay Program offers guided and self-guided local tours; guided tours require at least 5.5 hours and an advance reservation, normally six days ahead.
Express trainHaneda: Tokyo Monorail to Hamamatsucho (~13 min) or the Keikyu Line to Shinagawa (~11–14 min). Narita: the Narita Express (N'EX) runs direct to Tokyo Station in about 53 minutes.
Transit hotelHaneda: the Royal Park Hotel's Transit wing (Terminal 3) sits genuinely airside on just an international boarding pass. Narita: the 9h capsule hotel (Terminal 2) is landside — using it means clearing Japanese immigration.
Luggage storageCoin lockers throughout both airports (¥300–¥1,000/day) plus a staffed baggage counter at Narita Terminal 2.
Transit visaMost nationalities can stay in the airside transit area without a visa at either airport. Actually leaving requires a Japan visa where one is normally needed, or a discretionary Shore Pass from the immigration officer — not guaranteed, and dependent on nationality.
Exit & re-entryLeaving either airport means full immigration and customs both ways; reported waits run from a few minutes to well over an hour with little predictability — budget at least 3 hours round trip if you plan to exit.
Seoul IncheonSouth Korea · ICN
Guide
Incheon runs its own guided city-tour program for transit passengers, alongside a free Korean-culture museum for anyone staying airside instead.
4–8hr planAirside: the free K-Culture Museum and Korean Cultural Street (hanbok try-ons, craft workshops). With a valid visa/K-ETA, the airport's own free Transit Tour program runs guided loops from one hour up to six, covering palaces and traditional markets.
Express trainAREX (Airport Railroad Express) runs nonstop to Seoul Station in about 43 minutes from Terminal 1, 51 minutes from Terminal 2.
Transit hotelThe airside Transit Hotel in Terminal 2 (opposite Gate 251) is reserved for passengers connecting between international flights, sold in 6-hour blocks. The landside Darakhyu Capsule Hotel sits in both terminals' transportation centers.
Luggage storageStaffed, insured counters in both terminals — T1 near Check-in Area N, T2 at Entrances 3 and 4.
Transit visaTransit-tour admission depends on passport and itinerary: visa-free nationals may need K-ETA unless temporarily exempt, while some passengers holding visas or residence for the US, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, or specified European countries can qualify under third-country transit rules. The airport also lists a general transit-tour route. Verify the current rule for your passport before booking.
Exit & re-entryImmigration runs anywhere from 15–20 minutes off-peak to 90+ minutes at peak (weekday afternoons, weekends, cherry blossom season). The Transit Tour requires formally entering Korea, so budget generously for both directions plus a security re-screening.
Middle East
Dubai InternationalUnited Arab Emirates · DXB
Guide
Terminal 3's duty-free, lounges and a gym/pool/spa club fill a shorter layover without leaving the building; the Metro puts the city itself within reach on a longer one.
4–8hr planIn-terminal: duty-free shopping, the G-Force Club (gym, pool, spa) and Zen Gardens, all in Terminal 3. With 5+ hours and an eligible passport, the Metro reaches the Burj Khalifa/Dubai Mall area for a genuine city visit.
Express trainNo separate express — the Dubai Metro Red Line runs directly from Terminals 1 and 3 to the Burj Khalifa/Dubai Mall area in about 23–27 minutes.
Transit hotelDubai International Hotel sits inside the Terminal 3 transit area itself (Concourses A/B/C), sold in 3-, 6-, 12- and 24-hour blocks without clearing immigration.
Luggage storage24-hour left-luggage counters at both Terminal 1 and Terminal 3 arrivals.
Transit visa50+ nationalities (UK, US, most of the EU, Australia, Canada and others) get visa-on-arrival and can walk straight into the city. Everyone else needs a pre-arranged 48- or 96-hour transit visa, and a handful of nationalities don't qualify for either — verify by passport.
Exit & re-entryLeaving the transit area is a full UAE entry — your passport is stamped and the clock starts on your visa. Immigration averages about 12 minutes but can run to an hour at peak; allow 60–75 minutes for a same-terminal connection, ~90 for cross-terminal.
Europe
Amsterdam SchipholNetherlands · AMS
Guide
A small Rijksmuseum gallery and the Airport Library sit airside between the lounges — one of the few hub airports where a genuine city trip is realistic on a mid-length layover, transit-visa rules permitting.
4–8hr planAirside: the Rijksmuseum's airport gallery and the Airport Library near Lounges 2–3 for a quick, free stop. With 6–7+ hours and no Airport Transit Visa requirement, the train into central Amsterdam (canals, Dam Square) fits comfortably.
Express trainNS trains run directly from Schiphol Station, beneath the terminal, to Amsterdam Centraal every ~10 minutes — about 15–17 minutes each way.
Transit hotelYOTELAIR Amsterdam Schiphol sits airside past security in Lounge 2, capsule-style, open only to transiting passengers. The landside Sheraton Amsterdam Airport connects directly but sits outside the secure zone.
Luggage storageCoin lockers both pre- and post-security (Kelderplein and Holland Boulevard); a staffed 24-hour counter on Level -1 handles larger bags for up to 30 days.
Transit visaAs a Schengen airport, Amsterdam requires a Schengen Airport Transit Visa for a defined list of nationalities even to stay airside without entering the Netherlands — holders of a valid US, Canada or Japan visa are typically exempt. Verify by passport.
Exit & re-entryLeaving the transit area means a full external Schengen border check each way. Schiphol's own guidance recommends a 2-hour buffer for a Schengen connection and 3 hours for a non-Schengen one.
FrankfurtGermany · FRA
Guide
Terminal 3 opened in 2026 and Terminal 2 is now closed. Frankfurt's regional rail station still puts the old town within one short ride; airport-only time is better spent in Terminal 1's ticketed Visitors' Center or the transit-area facilities.
4–8hr planWith 5+ hours and permission to enter Germany, take the S-Bahn to Römerberg old town. For airport-only time, Terminal 1's ticketed Visitors' Center offers an indoor multimedia airport experience; the former Terminal 2 Visitors' Terrace is unavailable while that terminal is closed.
Express trainS8 and S9 trains run from the regional station beneath Terminal 1 to Frankfurt Hauptbahnhof in roughly 12–15 minutes. The long-distance station serves intercity routes and is not the quickest choice for this short city hop.
Transit hotelMY CLOUD Transit Hotel sits airside in Terminal 1's non-Schengen transit area, booked by the hour. The landside Sheraton is adjacent to Terminal 1; Terminal 3 connects by the SkyLine train during the day and shuttle bus overnight.
Luggage storageUse the staffed baggage-storage counters in Terminal 1. Terminal 2 is closed, so ignore older listings that still direct passengers to Hall D there.
Transit visaThe same Schengen Airport Transit Visa rules apply as Amsterdam — a defined list of nationalities needs one even to stay airside, with exemptions for holders of a valid US, Canada or Japan visa. Verify by passport.
Exit & re-entryLeaving the transit area means German immigration and another security screening. Non-EU short-stay travelers are processed through the EU Entry/Exit System, so do not plan around anecdotal five-minute border times; allow a generous buffer in both directions.
