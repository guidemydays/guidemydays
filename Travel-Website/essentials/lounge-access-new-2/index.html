Does the fee earn itself back?

Four ways into an airport lounge — flying business, paying at the door, carrying a membership, or carrying the right credit card — and what each one actually costs once you look past the headline benefit. The families

The four ways in

Same four families the lounge directory uses — here's what each actually costs.

Airline & alliance

Your cabin on the day, or your alliance status. Costs nothing extra once you have the ticket or the status — the trade-off is that a membership card never opens these doors, no matter which one you carry.

Pay at the door

No ticket class, no card, no membership — anyone can buy in. About $25–$100 for two to three hours, and booking online through the lounge's own site or an app is reliably cheaper than paying at the desk.

Membership

Priority Pass is the widest network; LoungeKey and DragonPass cover many of the same rooms under a different bank's branding. Priced in the machine above. Busy lounges turn members away before ticketed passengers.

Credit card

A network the issuer owns outright (Amex Centurion) or a bundled Priority Pass membership (most other cards). Almost always needs a same-day boarding pass — see the rules below.

Every lounge the site covers, airport by airport, worldwide, lives at /essentials/lounges/ — that page answers which lounges exist here and what does this one take. This one answers which way in is worth paying for.

The cards

What the major travel cards actually give you

Beyond the headline "lounge access included" line — and what changed in 2026.

American Express Platinum

Centurion Lounge · Global Lounge Collection · Delta Sky Club
Entry needs the physical card, a same-day boarding pass, and government ID. Delta Sky Club: access on same-day Delta-operated flights, capped at 10 visits a year unless you spend $75,000 or more on the card in a calendar year (then unlimited on eligible days); a guest costs $50 ($25 at Grab and Go locations), a child age 2–17 costs $30. Centurion Lounges: a guest costs $50 ($30 for a child, free under 2); hitting the same $75,000 threshold unlocks two complimentary guests per visit instead.
  • JUL 2026Since July 8, 2026: you must arrive within 3 hours of departure on a single flight, or within 5 hours of a connecting flight's departure — and any guest must be booked on the same flight as you.

Chase Sapphire Reserve

Priority Pass Select · Chase Sapphire Lounges by The Club
Priority Pass Select — 1,300+ lounges worldwide — activates automatically for the primary cardholder and every authorized user, alongside Chase's own branded Sapphire Lounges, select Air Canada Maple Leaf Lounges & Cafés, and the Etihad Lounge at Washington Dulles (IAD). Most locations admit two guests; Maple Leaf admits one. A layover stop at a Chase Sapphire Lounge is good for up to 5 hours before your connecting flight (raised from 3).
  • AUG 2026As of August 15, 2026, Chase Sapphire Lounges stopped accepting outside Priority Pass and LoungeKey members — a standalone Priority Pass card no longer gets a non-Chase cardholder through that specific door. This does not change anything for a Sapphire Reserve cardholder: your own access to Chase Sapphire Lounges and your own Priority Pass Select membership for every other lounge in the network are both unaffected.

Capital One Venture X

Capital One Lounges · Landings · full Priority Pass network, unlimited
The cardholder and every authorized user get unlimited visits, on the card alone, to Capital One's own Lounges and Landings plus the full Priority Pass network — no separate membership fee.
  • FEB 2026Since February 1, 2026, guests are no longer complimentary in Priority Pass lounges on this card — a $35-per-visit guest fee now applies there. Spending $75,000 or more on the card in a calendar year unlocks two complimentary guests, but only at Capital One's own Lounges and Landings; Priority Pass lounges still charge $35 a guest regardless of spend.
  • EXCEPTIONThe Venture X Business card still brings two guests into Priority Pass lounges free.
The fine print

Rules that catch people out

None of these is a price. All of them are why someone gets turned away at the desk holding the right card.

01

Delta Sky Club needs a same-day boarding pass — always

Every access method — Delta status, a membership, or a co-branded card — requires a same-day boarding pass on a Delta-operated or eligible SkyTeam partner flight. Entry runs from up to 3 hours before departure, or during a same-day connection. There is no walk-in option, membership or not.

02

Basic Economy is excluded from Delta Sky Club

A Basic Economy ticket is turned away at Delta Sky Club regardless of status, membership, or card — the fare class itself is the block, not the access method.

03

Amex now checks your arrival time and your guest's flight

Since July 8, 2026: Centurion Lounges and Delta Sky Clubs (via the Platinum Card) require arrival within 3 hours of a single flight's departure, or 5 hours of a connecting flight — and any guest must be ticketed on the same flight as you.

04

Guest fees moved, they didn't disappear

Capital One Venture X guests stopped being free in Priority Pass lounges on February 1, 2026 ($35/visit now). Amex's $75,000 spend threshold buys complimentary guests at Centurion Lounges and Delta Sky Club — but they're two separate thresholds, not one.

The verdict

Which one's worth it, by how often you fly

A rough frequency guide — not a recommendation to open a card for lounge access alone.

1–2trips a year
Pay at the door
An annual membership fee never earns itself back at that pace — two or three door visits a year costs less than any Priority Pass tier.
3–6trips a year
Priority Pass Standard Plus, or a card that already bundles Priority Pass Select
10 included visits roughly matches this range; if you'd carry a card like Chase Sapphire Reserve anyway for its other benefits, its bundled membership costs nothing extra.
7–15trips a year
Priority Pass Prestige, or Capital One Venture X
Unlimited visits stop being a luxury once per-visit fees would otherwise add up past the annual fee; Venture X gives the same unlimited entry as a card perk rather than a separate membership.
15+trips a year
American Express Platinum
Also the pick if you want the widest premium network. Centurion Lounges are the largest lounge network any single card owns outright, plus Delta Sky Club on Delta flights — at the cost of the same-day-boarding-pass rule and per-guest fees below the $75,000 spend threshold.
