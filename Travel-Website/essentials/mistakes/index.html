First-timer Mistakes

One or two errors visitors reliably make on a first trip to each place — booking the wrong season, staying in the wrong part of town, skipping a reservation that sells out months ahead, cramming too much into a day, or underestimating how far apart things are. Not safety or scams: those live on the Safety Guide. These are the mistakes that cost you a day, not your wallet.

Western Europe
ParisFrance
Book aheadTurning up at the Louvre, Eiffel Tower, or Sainte-Chapelle without a timed slot. All three sell out days ahead in season and the walk-up line can run two hours. Catacombs slots go weeks out.
Over-packedStacking the Louvre and Musée d'Orsay into one day. The Louvre alone is a half-day minimum; pairing two major museums means you rush both and enjoy neither.
LondonUnited Kingdom
Wrong baseBooking near Heathrow or in outer zones to save money. The nightly saving disappears into two hours of daily Tube time — stay in Zone 1 or the near edge of Zone 2.
DistancesReading the Tube map as a real map. It is a schematic and badly distorts distance — Leicester Square to Covent Garden is one stop and a three-minute walk.
AmsterdamNetherlands
Wrong baseStaying on or beside the Red Light District for the central location. It is loud until dawn every night of the week — the Jordaan and De Pijp are ten minutes away and quiet.
Wrong seasonBooking late April for the tulips. Keukenhof peaks mid-April and the bulb fields are usually cut by the first week of May.
BarcelonaSpain
Book aheadAssuming Sagrada Família and Park Güell can be done on the day. Both are capacity-capped timed entry and routinely sell out a week or more ahead in summer.
Closing daysPlanning a big lunch at 12:30. Kitchens mostly open 1:30–4pm and dinner rarely starts before 8:30pm — arriving at Spanish opening times, not your own, changes the whole trip.
MadridSpain
Wrong seasonVisiting in August. Madrid regularly sits near 40°C and much of the city — including many of the best restaurants — closes while locals leave.
DistancesTreating Toledo, Segovia, and Ávila as one combined day trip. Each is a comfortable half-day from Madrid; all three in a day is six hours of transit.
SevilleSpain
Wrong seasonBooking July or August. Daytime temperatures pass 42°C and sightseeing outdoors becomes genuinely unpleasant — March to May and October are the windows.
Closing daysLanding during Semana Santa or Feria without knowing. Both are extraordinary to witness, but hotels triple in price and normal sightseeing largely stops.
LisbonPortugal
Over-packedUnderestimating the hills. Lisbon is built on seven of them and a day that looks flat on a map involves serious climbing — plan roughly two-thirds of what you would elsewhere.
Book aheadGoing to Sintra without a Pena Palace time slot. Entry is timed, the bus queues are long, and unticketed visitors are regularly turned away in summer.
PortoPortugal
Wrong baseBooking in Vila Nova de Gaia expecting to be "in Porto". It is across the river — fine, and the port lodges are there, but every evening ends with a bridge crossing.
DistancesAssuming the Douro Valley is a quick hop. The good stretch is two hours each way by road; it needs a full day or an overnight, not an afternoon.
DublinIreland
Over-packedGiving Dublin five days. The core sights are comfortably two — the rest of the trip belongs on the west coast or in Wicklow.
Closing daysPlanning a Sunday around shops and smaller museums. Many keep short Sunday hours or close entirely, and Good Friday still shuts a lot of the city.
EdinburghUnited Kingdom
Wrong seasonArriving in August without realising the Fringe is on. The city's population roughly doubles, accommodation prices follow, and anything unbooked is gone.
DistancesPlanning Loch Ness or Skye as a day trip. Skye is five hours each way — the Highlands need their own two or three days.
NiceFrance
Wrong baseBasing in Monaco or Cannes and commuting. Nice has the airport, the rail hub, and the cheapest beds on the coast — everything else is a short train ride from it.
Wrong seasonComing in August, when the whole of France is also on the Riviera. June and September have the same sea temperature and half the crowds.
CopenhagenDenmark
Closing daysPlanning around Tivoli in winter or early spring. It closes between seasons and only reopens for set periods — check the calendar before building a day around it.
DistancesNot realising Malmö in Sweden is 35 minutes away by train. First-timers repeatedly miss an easy second country on a short trip.
StockholmSweden
Wrong seasonBooking December for the light. Stockholm gets around six hours of daylight in midwinter, and it is dim daylight — June gives you eighteen.
Wrong baseStaying in Gamla Stan for the postcard. It is beautiful, cramped, and empties of locals after dark — Södermalm and Norrmalm are better bases.
ViennaAustria
Book aheadExpecting to see the Spanish Riding School or a Musikverein concert on a whim. Both sell out well in advance, and the standing-room tricks only work if you know the timings.
Closing daysBuilding a Monday around museums. Several of the big ones close that day, and most shops shut entirely on Sundays.
BerlinGermany
Over-packedTreating Berlin like a compact European capital. It is nine times the area of Paris — pick two districts a day, not six sights.
Wrong baseStaying around Alexanderplatz or Potsdamer Platz. Both are transit hubs with little life after work — Kreuzberg, Neukölln, and Prenzlauer Berg are where the city actually is.

BudapestHungary
Wrong seasonSkipping winter because of the cold. The thermal baths are at their best in the snow, and hotel prices are a fraction of summer.
Closing daysArriving at a bath at midday on a weekend. Széchenyi and Gellért are packed 11am–5pm; early morning is calm and half-empty.
