Night Markets

The stalls don't open until sunset, and the best dish is the one with the longest local queue.

Ten evening street-food markets across eight fleet cities — Bangkok, Taipei, Hong Kong, Marrakech, Singapore, Chiang Mai, Luang Prabang and Shanghai — with the neighbourhood, the best time to arrive, what's actually worth queuing for, whether the stalls take cards, a pickpocket-risk tier, and one tourist trap to skip. This covers evening street-food scenes that run on a fixed nightly or weekly clock, not a one-off event; for daytime weekly produce and flea markets, see Market Days, and for annual festivals and named events, see Best Festivals & Holidays. Every entry links to its city guide.

Pickpocket risk:
BangkokThailand
Jodd Fairs (RCA)Low risk
Thursday–Sunday · roughly 16:00–00:00 · arrive by 18:00, before the queues build
Rama IX, next to Central Rama 9
Queue for: grilled river prawns, boat noodles, giant banana roti crepes, lobster
Cash preferred · PromptPay QR at most stalls
Skip: the clothing stalls ringing the entrance are priced for passersby — the food lanes further in are where the real queues (and the better prices) are.
Rot Fai Night Market RatchadaLow risk
Thursday–Sunday · roughly 17:00–00:00 · arrive by 18:00–19:00
Din Daeng, behind Esplanade Ratchadaphisek
Queue for: grilled pork skewers (moo ping), wok-tossed pad thai, mango sticky rice
Cash only at most stalls
Skip: the vintage-clothing and "antique" stalls mark up hard for a first offer — haggling down 30–40% here is normal, not rude.
TaipeiTaiwan
Shilin Night MarketLow risk
Daily · roughly 16:00–00:00 · arrive by 18:00–19:00, ahead of the after-dinner crowd
Shilin District, by Jiantan MRT (not Shilin MRT)
Queue for: large fried chicken cutlet, oyster omelet, bubble tea, stinky tofu
Cash at most stalls · EasyCard and mobile pay at the larger vendors
Skip: a few stalls near the MRT exit sell a noticeably smaller cutlet at the same price as the famous oversized one — check the size before ordering, not just the sign.
Hong KongHong Kong
Temple Street Night MarketModerate risk
Daily · roughly 18:00–00:00, busiest 20:00–22:00 · arrive after 19:00
Yau Ma Tei / Jordan, Kowloon
Queue for: clay-pot rice, curry fish balls, typhoon-shelter crab at the dai pai dong seafood stalls
Cash strongly preferred; few stalls take cards
Skip: unlicensed fortune tellers and tailors along the street quote a low headline price then upsell hard once you're seated — agree the full price before anything starts.
MarrakechMorocco
Jemaa el-FnaHigh risk
Food stalls set up around sunset (18:00–19:00) · arrive soon after opening
Medina, the city's central square
Queue for: whichever numbered food stall has the longest local queue, snail soup (babbouche), grilled meats and kebabs, fresh orange juice from the surrounding carts
Cash only (Moroccan dirhams)
Skip: unsolicited henna artists and animal handlers (snakes, monkeys) will grab a hand or place an animal on a shoulder for a photo, then demand a large payment — don't stop, don't make eye contact, and agree any price before anyone touches you or your belongings.
SingaporeSingapore
Lau Pa Sat & Boon Tat Street Satay StallsLow risk
Hawker hall open 24 hours; satay street sets up from about 19:00 · arrive 19:30–20:00
Raffles Place, Downtown Core
Queue for: chicken and mutton satay grilled streetside, Hokkien mee, char kway teow
Cash and card/PayNow both widely accepted
Skip the miscount: count your satay sticks as they're served — a handful of Boon Tat Street stalls have a long-running history of over-counting on the bill.
Newton Food CentreLow risk
Daily · roughly 17:00–02:00 · arrive 18:30–19:30, ahead of the tour-bus crowd
Newton, near Newton MRT
Queue for: chili crab, satay, barbecued stingray in sambal
Cash and card both accepted
Skip the dispute: ask to see seafood weighed and the price confirmed before it's cooked — Singapore's consumer watchdog has repeatedly flagged a few stalls here for disputed by-weight pricing with tourists.
Chiang MaiThailand
Sunday Walking Street (Ratchadamnoen Road)Low risk
Sundays only · roughly 16:00–00:00 · arrive by 17:00, before the street fills
Old City, the length of Ratchadamnoen Road from Tha Phae Gate
Queue for: khao soi, sai oua (northern Thai herbed sausage), mango sticky rice from the temple-courtyard food courts along the route
Cash only at nearly every stall
Skip: silver-look jewelry sold along the strip is often a cheaper alloy, not sterling silver — buy from stalls with a visible hallmark stamp.
Luang PrabangLaos
Luang Prabang Night MarketLow risk
Daily · roughly 17:00–22:00 · arrive 17:30–18:00 for the food alley
Sisavangvong Road, closed to traffic each evening, toward Wat Xieng Thong
Queue for: the food-stall alley behind the old market building — grilled Mekong river fish, Lao sausage, sticky rice, and the all-you-can-eat "Lao buffet" stalls
Cash only (Lao kip; Thai baht and US dollars often accepted too)
Skip: handicrafts sold as "hand-woven local textiles" are frequently machine-made imports — look for an Ock Pop Tok or local weaving-cooperative tag if authenticity matters to you.
ShanghaiChina
Yuyuan BazaarModerate risk
Shops run roughly 9:00–21:00, but the surrounding food streets stay busy into the evening · arrive 17:00–18:00, after the day-trip groups thin out
Old City, around Yu Garden and the City God Temple
Queue for: xiaolongbao soup dumplings (the line at Nanxiang Steamed Bun Restaurant is normal), scallion pancake, crab roe dumplings
WeChat Pay and Alipay near-universal; carry some cash as backup, foreign cards rarely work at stalls
Skip: a classic "tea ceremony" scam operates near here — a friendly local invites you to a nearby tea house for a tasting, then presents a bill for hundreds of dollars. Never follow a stranger's invitation to a tea tasting.
