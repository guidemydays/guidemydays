Key Things to Know
Apply 9–12 months before your next international trip
Routine processing commonly runs 6–12 weeks door-to-door depending on your country's own passport authority. Don't wait until 6 months out — if demand spikes, you may not make it in time without paying an expedited fee, where one is even offered.
The 6-month rule catches people off guard
Your passport may technically be valid, but if it has less than 6 months remaining after your return date, many countries will turn you away at the border — and airlines will deny boarding.
Child passports usually expire sooner than adult ones
Commonly 5 years rather than 10, though the exact figure and whether both parents/guardians must appear in person varies by country. Check your own passport authority's requirements for minors well before applying.
Count your blank pages before every trip
Many countries require at least 2–4 blank pages. Most passport authorities no longer offer a page-adding service (the US discontinued its version some years ago) — a full passport usually means renewing rather than adding pages.
Some visas require a full blank page
Countries like India, Brazil, and South Africa require a full blank page just for their visa stamp. Factor this in before trips that need visas stamped in-person.
Make a copy before every trip
Store a photo of your passport data page in your email or cloud storage. If your passport is stolen abroad, having the number and issue date speeds up emergency replacement at the nearest embassy of your own country.
Register your trip with your own government, if it offers the option
A number of countries run a free traveler-registration program that lets your embassy reach you in an emergency — the US's version is STEP (step.state.gov); Australia runs a similar one. Check whether your own country offers one before you travel; it typically takes two minutes.
Third-party appointment services are not your government
Any site charging a fee to "book your passport appointment" is a third party — official passport appointments are free directly through your own country's passport authority (in the US, that's travel.state.gov or 877-487-2778).
No AI-edited or digitally altered photos
Passport authorities increasingly screen for AI-generated and edited images — the US State Department is one that actively does. Submit an original, unedited photo taken within the last 6 months, whatever country you're applying to; altered photos are rejected.
