Global & regional networks

Which app covers what, and why the default changes when you cross a border.

Uber
The default across most of the fleet
The primary app in 148of 232 fleet cities below — the Americas, most of Western Europe, Australia, the UK, and large parts of South and Southeast Asia. Almost always accepts a foreign phone number at sign-up (the verification code arrives by SMS, with a voice-call fallback where SMS is blocked), which is why it stays the practical default even on a first trip somewhere new.
Bolt
Europe & Africa · typically 15–25% cheaper than Uber
The primary app in 18fleet cities — strongest across Eastern and Central Europe and parts of Africa, and available as a secondary option in many Western European cities where Uber is also present. Estonia-based; operates in 50+ countries worldwide. Worth opening alongside Uber in any European city to compare fares.
Grab
The default across Southeast Asia
The primary app in 8fleet cities — Thailand, Vietnam, Singapore, Malaysia, the Philippines and Indonesia. Also bundles food delivery and, in some cities, digital payments in the same app. Uber sold its Southeast Asian operations to Grab in 2018, so Grab is the regional successor rather than a competitor there.

City- or country-specific apps
35 fleet cities have their own local app instead

None of the global networks cover these places, so the local app is the only app-based option; search below for the specific city to see which one and how it works. No app? Do this instead

9 places in the fleet where no ride-hailing app operates at all — and what people actually use there.

Aruba
Aruba
No ride-hailing app operates in Aruba — the taxi trade is licensed and capped by the government, so neither Uber nor any competitor is on the island. Rides are licensed taxis on published fixed fares, the Arubus route along the hotel strip, a rental car, or your hotel’s own shuttle.
Havana
Cuba
No ride-hailing apps operate in Havana — Uber, Bolt, and similar services do not cover Cuba. State taxis — yellow metered Cubataxi cabs — are the most reliable option for tourists; agree on the fare before you ride or ensure the meter is running. Bicitaxis are plentiful in Old Havana for short hops. Private taxi drivers negotiated at your hotel are the practical choice for the Viñales day trip.
Bora Bora
French Polynesia
No ride-hailing apps operate on the island. Transfers between the main motu and Vaitape use resort boats and hotel shuttles; bicycles and scooters are rentable from local operators in Vaitape for exploring the main island road.
Zakynthos
Greece
No rideshare apps operate on Zakynthos; a rental car is the standard way to reach the island's beaches and viewpoints, and taxis can be arranged through hotels or flagged in Zakynthos Town for shorter journeys.
Pisa
Italy
No ride-hailing app operates in Pisa — Uber, Bolt and FreeNow stop at Florence. Taxis come from the CoTaPi cooperative: the ranks outside Pisa Centrale and at Piazza dei Miracoli, or a car booked through the hotel desk, which is a five-minute wait most of the day.
Petra
Jordan
No ride-hailing app operates in Wadi Musa — Uber and Careem stop at Amman and Aqaba. Taxis are hailed on Tourism Street or booked through the hotel desk, fares are agreed before setting off, and the Mövenpick sits a two-minute walk from the visitor centre gate.
Machu Picchu
Peru
Aguas Calientes is entirely pedestrian — there are no cars, motorbikes or ride-hailing apps in the town. Every stop in the guide is reachable on foot. The only motorised option is the Consettur shuttle bus that runs between the town centre and the ruins entrance.
Orcas Island
United States
No rideshare apps operate on Orcas Island. Visitors travel by rental car — book before arrival — the island's taxi service · Orcas Island Taxi · cash preferred, or the resort shuttle for guests.
Yellowstone
United States
No rideshare apps operate within the park; visitors navigate by rental car or guided tour, and the park's concessionaire runs shuttle services between some thermal areas during peak season.
