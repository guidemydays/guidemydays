Reservation culture varies more by region than almost anything else in wine tourism.

A policy that's optional in one valley is the only way in at another. Each card below covers what to expect showing up cold, what a standard tasting runs, how visitors actually get between estates, and what it takes to bring bottles home.

Napa Valley
United States (California)
Cellar door — closed

Most Napa producers now run on an appointment-only system, and allocation-only estates like Screaming Eagle or Harlan book out months ahead. Only a handful of large, visitor-oriented wineries — Robert Mondavi, Beringer, Castello di Amorosa among them — still keep walk-in tasting rooms with regular hours.

Typical tasting cost$40–$75 per person, often credited toward a bottle purchase; premium or library tastings run $100–$250+
Getting thereNo public transit between wineries — hire a driver, book a wine-country shuttle, or ride the Napa Valley Wine Train; California's DUI limit is 0.08% and checkpoints are common on Highway 29
Shipping bottles homeMost wineries ship within the US through a licensed carrier, but a few states (Utah among them) block direct-to-consumer wine shipments outright — check your home state's rules before buying a case

Burgundy
France
Cellar door — closed

Grand Cru growers along the Route des Grands Crus are almost all by appointment, frequently booked by email weeks ahead, while the négociant cellars in Beaune itself — Patriarche, the Marché aux Vins — run paid walk-in tastings daily.

Typical tasting costFree to €25 at the larger Beaune cellars; small grower visits increasingly charge €20–€50 given demand for a limited allocation
Getting thereVillages sit close together but have almost no public transit between them — a car, a private driver, or biking the flat Route des Grands Crus voie verte cycle path are the three real options

Chianti
Italy (Tuscany)
Cellar door — closed

Mid-size estates often take walk-ins outside peak season, but a tasting is usually bundled with a light lunch or a full pairing menu — and that part does need a booking, especially at agriturismo properties that also run a restaurant.

Typical tasting cost€15–€40 for a standard 3–4 wine tasting, often served with crostini, cheese or olive oil; multi-course pairing lunches run €60–€120+
Getting thereThe Chianti hills are poorly served by trains or buses — a rental car is the standard, or a private driver given Italy's low 0.05% BAC drink-driving limit
Shipping bottles homeEU shipping is straightforward; exporting to the US or elsewhere typically goes through a third-party wine-shipping company most estates already work with — ask before buying more than fits in a suitcase

Barossa Valley
Australia (South Australia)
Cellar door — closed

Large, well-known cellar doors — Jacob's Creek, Seppeltsfield, Penfolds among them — run regular walk-in hours with no booking needed. It's the small family producers and any private barrel-room tasting that require calling ahead.

Typical tasting costFree–A$15
Typical tasting costOften free or a nominal A$5–A$15, waived with a purchase; reserve or library tastings run A$25–A$60
Getting thereAbout an hour's drive from Adelaide with no rail service into the valley — self-drive (Australia drives on the left) or a full-day guided tour from Adelaide, useful given the 0.05% BAC limit
Shipping bottles homeDomestic shipping is easy through the cellar door or a courier; international shipping usually runs through a specialist export agent, and your home country's duty-free alcohol allowance still applies on arrival

Mendoza
Argentina
Cellar door — closed

Most of the well-known estates — Catena Zapata, Zuccardi, Bodega Salentein among them — run tastings and lunches strictly by appointment, particularly in the Uco Valley. Only a few larger, commercial wineries closer to the city allow walk-ins.

Typical tasting costUS$15–$40
Typical tasting costUS$15–$40 for a standard tasting; pairing lunches at top Uco Valley estates run $80–$200+ per person
Getting thereWineries spread across Luján de Cuyo, Maipú and the Uco Valley (up to 90 minutes from the city) with almost no public transit — rent a bike for the flatter Maipú wineries, or hire a driver for the day rather than self-driving between tastings
Shipping bottles homeSome estates can arrange international shipping through a consolidator, though cost and reliability vary; most visitors instead pack bottles carefully in checked luggage and clear their destination country's duty-free allowance on arrival

Stellenbosch
South Africa (Cape Winelands)
Cellar door — closed

Plenty of smaller, informal cellar doors take walk-ins any day they're open, though estates built around a restaurant — Delaire Graff, Tokara among them — prefer or require a booking, especially for a lunch pairing.

Typical tasting cost≈US$3–$8
Typical tasting costRoughly R50–R150 (about US$3–$8) at most estates, well below Napa or Burgundy prices; premium tastings and food pairings run R250–R600+
Getting thereAbout 45 minutes from Cape Town with no train service between estates — self-drive is affordable and standard, or a private driver given South Africa's strict 0.05% BAC limit
Shipping bottles homeDomestic courier shipping is simple; several estates work with Stellenbosch-based wine-export specialists for shipments to the US, UK or EU, though cost per bottle rises steeply on small orders — most visitors just pack bottles home instead

Before you buy a case

Every home country sets its own duty-free allowance for wine brought back from abroad, and it's usually far lower than a good tasting can tempt you into — a bottle or two typically clears customs without a second look, a case usually won't. Check your own country's personal allowance before committing to shipping or hand-carrying more than that.
