2.Agra Fort
A red sandstone fortress-palace on the Yamuna's west bank, the primary Mughal residence until 1638 and later the place where Shah Jahan was held under house arrest by his son Aurangzeb, confined to rooms with a view of the Taj Mahal he built; a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

1.Ram Bagh
India's oldest surviving Mughal garden, laid out on the Yamuna's east bank in 1526 by Babur, founder of the Mughal dynasty, who first called it Bagh-i-Gul Afshan; his body rested here before being carried to Kabul for burial, and its terraced, water-channeled charbagh plan became the template for every Mughal garden that followed.

2.Chini Ka Rauza
A riverside mausoleum built between 1628 and 1639 for Afzal Khan Shirazi, a Persian poet and minister at Shah Jahan's court, sheathed in glazed blue, turquoise and yellow tilework — chini, "China ware" — found nowhere else on an Agra monument.

3.Itmad-ud-Daulah's Tomb
Nicknamed the "Baby Taj," this jewel-box mausoleum was commissioned in 1622 by empress Nur Jahan for her father Mirza Ghiyas Beg; the first Mughal structure built entirely of white marble with pietra dura inlay, it is widely considered the design draft for the Taj Mahal that followed it.

1.Fatehpur Sikri
Emperor Akbar's purpose-built capital, raised in red sandstone from 1571 and abandoned within 15 years once its water supply failed; a UNESCO World Heritage Site whose palace complex — the five-tiered Panch Mahal, the carved single-pillar Diwan-i-Khas, Jodha Bai's Palace — survives almost exactly as the court left it.

The mausoleum of Emperor Akbar, greatest of the Mughal rulers, begun by Akbar himself and completed by his son Jahangir in 1613 in the suburb of Sikandra; a four-tiered pyramid of red sandstone terraces rising to an open marble pavilion, a design found nowhere else among the Mughal tombs.

Worth Knowing

A few things that make an Agra trip go smoother

Foreign visitors pay a one-time ₹500 ADA — Agra Development Authority — toll that covers same-day entry to the Taj Mahal, Agra Fort, Fatehpur Sikri, Akbar's Tomb and Itmad-ud-Daulah's Tomb together — the toll is waived on Fridays. Keep the receipt; gate staff at each site check it.

Photography is not allowed inside the Taj Mahal's central mausoleum chamber, where Shah Jahan and Mumtaz Mahal's cenotaphs sit — phones and cameras are fine everywhere else on the grounds. Large bags, tripods, food and smoking materials are barred at the security check and have no storage facility at the gates, so leave them at the hotel.

The Taj Mahal's ticket line moves fastest at the East Gate for foreign-ticket holders; the West Gate and South Gate serve mostly Indian-ticket visitors and domestic tour groups, and queue length there can run 30–45 minutes longer at midday.
