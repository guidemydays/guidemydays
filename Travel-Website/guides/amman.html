Worth Knowing

Amman rewards a slower pace than its day-trip neighbours suggest.

Friday is the quiet day: government sites keep their usual hours but King Abdullah I Mosque closes to visitors, and much of downtown does not properly open until early afternoon after Friday prayers.

Amman is built across seven hills, now closer to twenty, and street numbering restarts on every hill — a taxi driver navigates by circle number and landmark, not by house number, so give the nearest circle or a well-known building when explaining where you're going.

The city has no metro or tram; Uber and Careem cover the gaps between the numbered circles, and neither costs much by international standards.

Bring cash in small denominations, 1 and 5 JOD notes, for downtown eateries like Hashem, which does not take cards, and for tipping drivers.

Petra and the Dead Sea both make for very long single days from Amman — pack water, sun protection, and comfortable shoes each time, and expect an early start.
