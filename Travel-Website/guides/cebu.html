Local Tastes
Lechon Cebu
A whole pig slow-roasted over charcoal until the skin shatters and the meat stays juicy, seasoned only with native herbs and no soy or liver sauce — the style Anthony Bourdain called the best pig he'd ever eaten.
Zubuchon · House of Lechon · CnT Lechon
Dried Mangoes
Cebu's signature pasalubong export — ripe local mangoes sliced and sun- or machine-dried to a sweet, chewy strip, sold loose by the kilo or in branded packs across the city's markets.
Taboan Public Market
Ngohiong
A Cebuano-Chinese street snack of ground pork and jicama seasoned with five-spice, wrapped in a thin tofu skin and deep-fried to a crisp shell, served sliced with a sweet-spicy dipping sauce.
Larsian BBQ Market

Getting Around
Grab
Foreign number: accepted. — the sign-up country-code selector takes any country, and the OTP arrives by international SMS within seconds of requesting it.
Uber does not operate in the Philippines — Grab is the only ride-hailing app in Cebu, covering the whole metro from Mactan to downtown. Fares show upfront before booking, a real advantage over flagging a taxi. Surge pricing and driver cancellations are common at rush hour and in heavy rain.
grab.com/ph/transport
Worth Knowing

A few things that make sense once you're there

Magellan's Cross and the Basilica sit thirty seconds apart because they're one story split in two: Magellan planted the original cross in April 1521, and the carved Santo Niño image he gave Cebu's first Christian convert as a baptismal gift survived a village fire decades later to become the oldest Christian relic in the Philippines — the reason Cebu, not Manila, calls itself the birthplace of Philippine Christianity.

Grab covers ride-hailing and food delivery, but jeepneys, wet markets and most Colon Street and Carbon Market vendors are still cash-only — GCash is spreading fast through the malls and chain restaurants of IT Park and Ayala Center but isn't yet standard at street level in the older downtown core, so small peso bills matter more there than around the hotel.

Several of this guide's day tours pass through Oslob, where boatmen hand-feed wild whale sharks close to shore to guarantee sightings — a practice marine biologists have flagged for altering the animals' natural feeding and migratory behavior. It's still fully legal and heavily booked; travelers weighing the ethics sometimes look at open-water encounters elsewhere in the Philippines instead.
