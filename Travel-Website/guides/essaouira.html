1.Bab Marrakech at the Medina of Essaouira
The monumental southern gate opens onto Essaouira’s unusually orderly medina, making it the clearest place to read the old city’s eighteenth-century plan. Its round defensive tower now works as an exhibition space, while the broad stone approach makes the gate an easy orientation point for the medina.

Essaouira’s main public square links the medina lanes with the fishing harbour, with arcades, cafés and a broad Atlantic-facing promenade. Café terraces face the old customs houses, and the evening promenade is one of the best places to watch the working port settle into its social rhythm.

The carved Marine Gate forms the ceremonial entrance to the historic harbour and frames the compact shipyard, fishing fleet and fortified walls. The eighteenth-century stonework bears the royal inscription of Sidi Mohammed ben Abdallah and still funnels all foot traffic into the working port.

Fresh water meets the Atlantic at a broad tidal channel used by shorebirds, separating Essaouira’s main beach from the dunes of Diabat. Seasonal water levels draw shorebirds and reshape the walking line; keep to firm sand and do not attempt the crossing when the tide is rising.

Getting Around
No verified ride app, tram or passenger ferry carries local trips in Essaouira; walk inside the medina and use metered petit taxis or a hotel-arranged car beyond it.
Heads Up
Atlantic coast — Wind and tides
Carry a light layer, secure hats, and check the tide before walking the Oued Ksob shore or approaching Borj El Baroud.
Worth Knowing

Small cafés, taxis and market stalls often prefer Moroccan dirhams. Carry coins and small notes; cards are more reliable at hotels and polished restaurants.

The walled centre is largely pedestrian. Ask drivers to use Bab Marrakech or Bab Sbaa as the nearest practical drop-off, then continue on foot.

The fishing port is liveliest in the morning as boats unload. Wear shoes with grip and give working carts, nets and fish sellers room.

The trade winds make the bay superb for wind sports but cooler than inland Morocco. A windproof layer is useful even on bright days.
