Day 1
From Hotel: 38 min · 11 min → National Monument
1.National Monument
Jakarta's independence monument rises above Merdeka Square, with a flame-topped tower and a museum recounting the struggle for nationhood. The observation deck gives a broad city view. Buy admission at the counter with JakCard; tower access is quota-limited and counters close one hour before the monument.

2.Istiqlal Mosque
Indonesia's national mosque has a vast dome, steel-clad columns and broad marble courts. Its independence-era symbolism rewards a guided visit. The listed opening hours cover worship access; tourist entry follows the visitor service's arrangements. Dress modestly, remove shoes where directed and follow staff instructions.

3.Lapangan Banteng
Walk beneath the trees to the West Irian Liberation Monument, whose outstretched figure rises above the park's pools and terraces. This former colonial parade ground now works as a public garden and gathering place; the shaded paths give the morning a quieter interval between Jakarta's monumental buildings.

4.National Museum of Indonesia
Stone Hindu-Buddhist sculpture and archaeological collections make this the essential museum for understanding the archipelago beyond Jakarta. Concentrate on the ancient statues and the displays tracing Indonesia's cultures rather than trying to exhaust every gallery. The national collection supplies the historical context for the regional architecture you will see later at Taman Mini.

Day 2
From Hotel: 16 min → Glodok
1.Glodok
Follow the older streets of Jakarta's Chinatown from Jalan Pancoran towards the temple lanes. Look above the shopfronts for tiled roofs, carved details and the close-packed fabric of a neighbourhood shaped by generations of Chinese Indonesian residents. Take time to explore the streets around Dharma Bhakti respectfully; this is a lived-in district with active places of worship.

2.Kota Tua Jakarta
Begin at Fatahillah Square, where the old town hall and colonial facades frame the centre of former Batavia. Continue towards Kali Besar to follow the canal-side streets and compare restored buildings with the surviving urban fabric. The reward is the district as a whole: broad civic spaces, narrow lanes and the relationship between the old city and its port.

3.Sunda Kelapa
The old harbour connects Jakarta's beginnings with a trade that still moves by sea. Walk along the permitted quays to see traditional wooden cargo vessels and the daily work around them. Stay clear of loading operations and enjoy the boats from land; boarding a vessel is not included in this visit.

1.Bogor Botanical Gardens
Follow shaded paths through palms, mature tropical trees and botanical collections around the presidential palace grounds. Allow time for the orchid collection and quieter garden corners. Use the visitor guide and current signs for closed paths. Leave by 4:00pm, then take a meal and rest break before the evening train.

Local Tastes
Soto Betawi
Jakarta’s rich beef soup combines meat, potatoes and tomatoes with a coconut-milk or milk-enriched broth. Lime and sambal sharpen its creamy flavour.
Soto Betawi H. Ma’ruf · Soto Betawi Haji Husein
Kerak Telor
A Betawi sticky-rice omelette cooked over charcoal, often inverted over the heat, then topped with toasted coconut, fried shallots and dried shrimp.
Bang Sape’i Kerak Telor
Nasi Uduk
Fragrant rice steamed with coconut milk and aromatics, served with fried chicken, tofu, tempeh and sambal. Small banana-leaf parcels are a Kebon Kacang speciality.
Kedai Zainal Fanani · Nasi Uduk Gondangdia

Heads Up
Thamrin — Sunday road access
Sunday car-free mornings can alter car collection points. Confirm the pickup point with reception before ordering a car.
Dufan — Two admission layers
An Ancol gate ticket does not include Dufan rides. Check that your package covers both admissions before travelling.
Worth Knowing

Jakarta’s Betawi culture grew from generations of people settling around the old port. Its food, language and performance traditions reflect that meeting of communities, so the city’s local identity is best understood as a blend rather than a single imported tradition.

Istiqlal’s architect, Friedrich Silaban, won a national design competition. His approach to tropical buildings favoured broad roofs and generous corridors, making shade and airflow part of the architecture rather than an afterthought.
