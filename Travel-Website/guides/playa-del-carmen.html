Uber Eats
Foreign number: not accepted. — the Mexico sign-up flow requires a +52 number and a Mexican-issued card; a foreign number's SMS code is rejected at the form, not merely unread.
Covers most of Centro and the hotel zone with a wide restaurant list, but a Mexican SIM or a local account is the only practical way in for a visiting foreigner.
ubereats.com/mx
Rappi
Foreign number: not accepted. — Rappi verifies only numbers issued by carriers in its live countries, so a foreign mobile number never receives the code at all.
The broadest platform in the city, covering groceries and pharmacies as well as restaurants, but the same local-number gate as Uber Eats blocks a fresh sign-up from abroad.
rappi.com.mx
Getting Around
Uber
Foreign number: accepted. — an account verified in your home country signs in and hails rides in Mexico without a new SMS code; only a brand-new sign-up needs a local number.
Widely available in Centro and Playacar, cheaper and more predictable than flagging a street taxi, though surge pricing hits hard around Xcaret's night-show closing time.
uber.com/mx
DiDi
Foreign number: accepted. — international numbers receive the SMS verification code normally through DiDi's own sign-up flow.
Often the cheaper of the two apps here, with a fare-bidding system that can undercut Uber on short hops around Centro, though the English interface is less polished.
didiglobal.com

Heads Up
Fifth Avenue — Timeshare pitches
Friendly strangers offering a free breakfast or tour discount are almost always timeshare sellers; a polite no and walking on works better.
Main beach — Sargassum season
Brown seaweed washes ashore April through October, worst in June and July; mornings are cleaner, and Cozumel's shoreline usually fares better.
Worth Knowing

A beach town built as a base, not a checklist

Playa del Carmen works best as a hub rather than a destination to exhaust on its own — the town itself is a walkable evening and a good beach, and the real draws, Xcaret, Tulum, the cenotes, sit thirty minutes to two hours out. Renting a car for the Cobá/Tulum day is genuinely worth pricing against a tour: Mexican rental agencies routinely quote a low online rate and then add mandatory insurance at the counter that can double the total, so a walk-in comparison at two or three agencies on 5th Avenue before booking anything in advance often saves money.

Bring pesos in cash for every archaeological site — Tulum and Cobá both charge a combined INAH/park fee at the gate, card readers there are unreliable, and there is no ATM at either site or at Gran Cenote. The nearest reliable ATMs are back in Playa del Carmen or Tulum town, not at the ruins themselves.

The ferry to Cozumel leaves right from the foot of 5th Avenue and the crossing is under 45 minutes — worth keeping in mind if the mainland beach is having a rough sargassum week, since the island's leeward coast is sheltered from the same currents that pile seaweed onto Playa del Carmen's own sand.
