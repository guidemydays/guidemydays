Food Delivery
Glovo
Foreign number: accepted. — the sign-up form takes any country code, and Glovo's own support pages confirm foreign numbers work across its full international network.
The largest delivery platform in Sofia by restaurant count, covering both Bulgarian kitchens and international chains; also delivers groceries and pharmacy items, which the other two apps here don't.
glovoapp.com
Wolt
Foreign number: accepted. — a Finnish-built app live in 25+ countries on one account and verification system, so a number verified elsewhere works in Sofia too.
Newer to Sofia than Glovo but reviewers rate its tracking and delivery-time accuracy highest of the three; the smallest restaurant count of the three apps here, concentrated in the city center.
wolt.com
Bolt Food
Foreign number: accepted. — runs on the same Bolt account and phone-verification system as Bolt's ride-hailing app, which already takes foreign numbers across its international footprint.
Launched later in Sofia than Glovo and covers mainly the central districts; useful as a backup when a restaurant is out of range on the other two apps, or to consolidate with an existing Bolt ride account.
bolt.eu
Getting Around
TaxiMe
Foreign number: accepted. — the sign-up flow takes any international number by SMS code, and the app runs fully in English.
Sofia's most-used taxi app, working only with vetted drivers charging standard metered city rates — the most reliable way to avoid the inflated fares some street taxis quote tourists.
taxime.to
Yellow Taxi
Foreign number: accepted. — the app interface and sign-up run in English and over a dozen other languages, taking international numbers by SMS code with no Bulgarian SIM needed.
Sofia's long-established taxi operator with the largest fleet in the city; reviewers report longer waits and more driver cancellations than TaxiMe at busy times.
yellow.bg

Worth Knowing

A capital that keeps every layer of its history in view at once

Bulgarian head gestures run backwards from most of the world's — a side-to-side shake traditionally means yes, an up-and-down nod means no. Many Sofia residents who deal with visitors switch to the familiar gesture automatically, which only adds a second layer of confusion; when in doubt, ask out loud.

Bulgaria uses the lev, not the euro, despite EU membership — it's pegged to the euro at a fixed rate, but euro cash is not accepted at most tills, so change money or draw levs from an ATM on arrival.

A single walk down Knyaginya Maria Luiza Boulevard passes a Roman street grid, an Ottoman mosque, a Sephardic synagogue and a Vienna Secession bathhouse within a few hundred metres of each other — few European capitals stack that many empires into one block.

Tipping isn't obligatory but is customary at sit-down restaurants — rounding up or adding roughly 10% in cash is the norm; card machines rarely offer a tip-line prompt.

Every metro barrier and tram or bus reader in Sofia takes a contactless bank card or phone tap directly — no ticket, no app, no Bulgarian cash needed. A single ride is €0.80 with a free transfer within 30 minutes, and repeated taps on the same card in one day cap out at €2.00 total.
