Local Tastes
Cochinita Pibil
Yucatán's signature slow-roast — pork marinated in sour orange and achiote, wrapped in banana leaf, and pit-roasted until it shreds. Served on tacos or tortas with pickled red onion.
Taqueria Honorio · El Xamach
Poc Chuc
Thin-pounded pork loin marinated in sour orange, char-grilled and finished with pickled onion and a side of black beans — one of the peninsula's oldest grilled dishes.
Taqueria Honorio · El Xamach
Panuchos
A fried tortilla pocket stuffed with refried black beans, then topped with shredded turkey or chicken, lettuce, tomato and pickled onion — a Yucatecan antojito rarely seen outside the peninsula.
Taqueria La Chiapaneca

inDrive
Foreign number: accepted. — verifies by SMS through Twilio's international routing, which reaches most foreign carriers without a local SIM.
The taxi union keeps Uber and DiDi from picking up local fares in Tulum, so inDrive is the ride app that actually works here — the rider names a price and nearby drivers accept or counter, which keeps fares honest on routes with no meter.

Worth Knowing

Two states, one trip

Day 5 crosses from Quintana Roo into Yucatán state, and the clock moves with it — Tulum keeps its own Southeast zone at UTC-5 year-round, while Valladolid and Ek Balam sit an hour behind on Yucatán's Central zone. A 9am departure from the hotel reads as 8am on arrival; plan the day's timing off the destination's clock, not the one on your phone at breakfast.

Mexican law keeps every beach public, but the loungers and shade structures on top of the sand almost always belong to a private beach club charging a minimum consumption instead of an entry fee — often well over $50 a person. A handful of hotels and clubs now offer genuinely free access with no spending requirement, but they are the exception rather than the rule, so confirm a club's policy before settling in for the day.
