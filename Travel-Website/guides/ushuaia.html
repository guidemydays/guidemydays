Food Delivery
PedidosYa
Foreign number: not accepted. — sign-up requires an Argentine mobile number; the form's own country-code selector will not complete verification on a foreign SIM.
The dominant delivery app in Ushuaia, covering most of the restaurants on San Martín and the waterfront with typical drop-offs inside 40 minutes; without a local SIM, ask the hotel front desk to place the order instead.
pedidosya.com.ar
Rappi
Foreign number: not accepted. — account creation requires a mandatory SMS one-time code sent to an Argentine number, with no email or WhatsApp fallback.
A smaller second option in Ushuaia with a thinner restaurant list than PedidosYa, useful mainly as a fallback when a specific place isn't listed on the larger app.
rappi.com.ar
Getting Around
Uber
Foreign number: accepted. — sign-in works on an existing home-country account and number; no local SIM or Argentine number is required.
Uber launched in Ushuaia only recently and the driver pool is still small, so wait times run longer than in Buenos Aires and a ride can be hard to find late at night; it is still the easiest way to book a car without calling ahead in Spanish.
uber.com
Heads Up
Glaciar Martial — No working chairlift
The chairlift has been out of service for years — reaching the viewpoint platform is a 40-minute uphill walk.
Isla Martillo — Landing groups are capped
Only 20 visitors are allowed on the island at a time, so penguin-walk tours sell out — book a few days ahead in peak season.

Worth Knowing

Small facts that make more sense of a very remote place

Ushuaia's port is the main departure point for Antarctica expedition cruises, so a ship docked at the pier most mornings between November and March is likely bound for the Antarctic Peninsula, not just the Beagle Channel.

The city's full name comes from the Yaghan language and is usually translated as "bay that penetrates to the west" — a reference to the harbor the town is built around, not to the "end of the world" branding seen everywhere downtown.

Tierra del Fuego owes its name to Ferdinand Magellan, who in 1520 saw fires lit by the Yaghan and Selk'nam people along the coast and named the archipelago "Land of Fire."

Argentina's currency situation means card exchange rates can lag well behind cash rates; many independent shops, cafés and tour operators still prefer or require cash in practice.

The prison that founded the city and the national park that now anchors most visits sit only a few kilometers apart — the same convict labor that built Ushuaia's early buildings also cut the rail line the End of the World Train now rides for pleasure.
