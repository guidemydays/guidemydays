Local Tastes
Gudeg
Young jackfruit stewed for hours in palm sugar and coconut milk until dark and sweet, served with rice, chicken, egg and spicy krecek — Yogyakarta's signature dish.
Gudeg Yu Djum Wijilan 31
Sate Klathak
Goat satay skewered on bicycle-spoke iron rods instead of bamboo, grilled over coconut-husk coals and seasoned only with salt, native to the Bantul area south of the city.
Sate Klathak Pak Pong
Bakpia
Small baked mung-bean pastries, originally a Chinese-Indonesian recipe adapted in the Pathuk neighborhood and sold in dozens of flavors from stalls near Malioboro.
Bakpia Pathuk stalls · Jalan Pathuk
Wedang Ronde
A hot ginger tea with chewy glutinous-rice balls, peanuts and grass jelly, sold from street carts in the evening as the temperature drops.
Street carts · Jalan Malioboro

Worth Knowing

A few things this guide learned the hard way, so the reader doesn't have to.

Around Malioboro and the Kraton, a friendly stranger who mentions a "batik exhibition only open today, students only" is running the city's best-known tourist scam — the gallery sells ordinary batik at inflated prices and the "exhibition" runs every single day. Politely decline and keep walking.

Yogyakarta's Sultan holds a role found nowhere else in Indonesia: Hamengkubuwono X is simultaneously the hereditary head of the Kraton and the elected-in-practice Governor of the Special Region, a dual position written into the region's own founding law rather than the national one.

Becak and andong drivers around Malioboro rarely run a meter — agree the fare in cash before getting in, and expect a detour past a batik or silver shop partway through if the price seemed unusually low.

Mount Merapi is one of Indonesia's most active volcanoes and its alert level changes with little warning; check the current status before booking a jeep tour, since routes on the mountain's flank close first when activity rises.

The Ramayana Ballet moves indoors to the Trimurti building from November through April and loses its open-air, temple-lit backdrop in the process — the outdoor stage only runs May through October.
