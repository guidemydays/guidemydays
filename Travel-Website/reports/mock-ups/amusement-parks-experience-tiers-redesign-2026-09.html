The world's best theme parks, from the historic fairgrounds that invented the form to the modern mega-resorts. The thing that decides your day is not the park but the queue.

Mega-Resort 7 parks Multi-day · Resort complex

Full-scale resort complexes where the park itself is the destination. Budget 1–3 days; the queue strategy matters more than the ticket price.

Tokyo DisneySea
Urayasu, Chiba — Japan
Mega-Resort 1–2 days recommended

Consistently rated the finest theme park on earth. Built entirely around a nautical-fantasy world with no recycled attractions from other Disney parks. Alcohol sold; adult atmosphere throughout.

Disneyland Paris
Marne-la-Vallée — France
Mega-Resort 1–2 days

Europe's largest theme-park resort, with two parks on one site. Phantom Manor — the Haunted Mansion variant — is arguably better than the California original. Off-peak October visits are significantly cheaper.

Universal Studios Japan
Osaka — Japan
Mega-Resort Full day · 1 day sufficient

Japan's Universal is operationally pristine — shorter waits than comparable US parks and exceptional food quality. The Wizarding World of Harry Potter and the Nintendo World area are both best-in-class.

Thrill Specialist 5 parks Coaster-first · Half to full day

Parks defined by their coaster lineup rather than IP or theming. Come for the rides; stay as long as the queue allows.

Fuji-Q Highland
Fujiyoshida, Yamanashi — Japan
Thrill Specialist Half day · 4–6 hours

Four world-record coasters against a backdrop of Mt. Fuji on clear days. The park is small and ride counts are low, but each is extreme. Strategically placed for a Tokyo day trip.

Canada's Wonderland
Vaughan, Ontario — Canada
Thrill Specialist Full day

The largest coaster collection in Canada. Within reach of Toronto by transit. Leviathan (93 m drop) and Yukon Striker are both top-tier steel coasters.

Character & Story 6 parks IP-led · Themed worlds

Parks where narrative immersion is the product — not thrill intensity. Suitable for visitors who do not ride roller coasters.

Efteling
Kaatsheuvel, North Brabant — Netherlands
Character & Story Full day · 1 day sufficient

Europe's most beautiful fairy-tale park, open since 1952. The original Fairytale Forest walk — hand-crafted scenes of Brothers Grimm stories — has no equivalent anywhere else in the world.

Puy du Fou
Les Epesses, Vendée — France
Character & Story 1–2 days · Evening show essential

Not a theme park in any conventional sense — a living history spectacle with 2,300 costumed volunteers. No rides. La Cinéscénie night show (14,000 seats) runs only on weekend evenings and is unlike anything else in Europe.

Discovery & Nature 4 parks Eco & water · Nature-led

Parks built around natural environments, wildlife, or cenote-swimming — fundamentally different from the manufactured thrills above.

Xcaret Park
Riviera Maya, Quintana Roo — Mexico
Discovery & Nature Full day · 8–10 hours

An eco-archaeological park in a natural inlet on the Caribbean. Cenote swimming, a night show, wildlife encounters, and Mexican cultural performances are all on one ticket.

Chimelong Ocean Kingdom
Hengqin, Zhuhai — China
Discovery & Nature Full day

The world's largest aquarium by water volume, embedded inside a theme park. The whale-shark exhibit — the largest of its kind — justifies the trip from Zhuhai or Macau alone.

Classic & Historic 5 parks 1800s–1970s · Atmosphere-first

Parks that predate the modern theme-park era — atmosphere, history, and craftsmanship over raw thrill or IP.

Tivoli Gardens
Copenhagen — Denmark
Classic & Historic Half day · Evening preferred

Open since 1843 and one of Walt Disney's acknowledged inspirations. The lantern-lit evening atmosphere has no modern equivalent. In the centre of Copenhagen, minutes from the central station.

Europa-Park
Rust, Baden-Württemberg — Germany
Classic & Historic 1–2 days

Europe's most attended theme park outside Disneyland Paris. Its 17 national-themed zones are built and operated by a single family, giving it a consistency of quality hard to find elsewhere.

Why this redesign
What the current flat grid buries — the reader choosing between Walt Disney World and Fuji-Q Highland is making two completely different decisions: one is a 3-day resort stay, the other is a 4-hour coaster trip. Today, both cards look identical. The Experience Tiers concept surfaces this instantly.
  • Sticky tier-jump nav — 5 pills, one click to the tier that matches intent. Country filter remains but is secondary. Replaces the current country dropdown as the primary navigation layer.
  • Horizontal strip cards — text-first (photo on the right). Name, location, and commitment pill are visible before reading the description.
  • 4px left-bar accent — the only tier-identity element per card. Cards stay white/paper throughout (NN 94: no black or vibrant fills).
  • Commitment pill — "Half day · 4–6 hours" or "1–2 days recommended" on every card. Currently buried inside descriptions and absent from many.
  • Two pills per card, never the same family — type pill matches the tier (cp-purple/cp-orange/cp-blue/cp-green/cp-fog); commitment pill always cp-fog, a distinct neutral (NN 102 compliant).
  • Badge font-size uses min(12px, 0.75em) — never larger than surrounding body text (NN x06).
  • No shared CSS changes — entire redesign lives in the page's own <style> block.
  • Search bar and country filter unchanged — both continue to work across all tiers as they do today.
