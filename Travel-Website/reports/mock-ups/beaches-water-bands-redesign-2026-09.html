Best Beaches

Beaches picked for the sand, water and setting rather than the resorts behind them. What you actually want depends on the water: reef-sheltered lagoons are calm, warm and shallow enough for children; long Atlantic strands are wide, wild and genuinely cold; volcanic black sand holds heat and can be too hot to cross barefoot by midday.

Reef & Lagoon ~32 entries Calm, warm, turquoise water — reef-sheltered, snorkelling within reach
Whitehaven Beach Australia LAGOON
A beach on an uninhabited Whitsunday island known for exceptionally fine, bright silica sand and the swirling sand-and-water patterns of Hill Inlet at its northern end.
Wikipedia Book Tour Map
Turquoise Bay Australia REEF SNORKEL
A beach in Cape Range National Park on the Ningaloo Coast. A fringing coral reef sits just offshore and can be reached by snorkellers straight from the sand.
Wikipedia WA Tourism Map
Half Moon Bay Antigua LAGOON
A crescent-shaped bay on Antigua's remote southeastern coast, enclosed by rocky headlands, with a calmer reef-sheltered end and a more exposed, surf-prone stretch.
Wikipedia Map
Eagle Beach Aruba LAGOON
A wide, low-density beach on Aruba's western coast, known for its soft white sand, gently shelving water, and the two weathered fofoti trees that stand at its edge.
Aruba Guide Wikipedia Map
Open Ocean ~28 entries Exposed swell, wide horizons — surf-prone, often cooler water
Bondi Beach Australia OPEN OCEAN
Sydney's best-known ocean beach — a wide sweep of sand in the eastern suburbs backed by a promenade and the start of the coastal walk to Coogee.
Sydney Guide Wikipedia Map
Copacabana Brazil OPEN OCEAN
A four-kilometre arc of sand in Rio's South Zone, backed by an undulating mosaic promenade and a constant mix of locals, volleyball games, and beach vendors.
Rio Guide Wikipedia Map
Praia do Camilo Portugal ATLANTIC STRAND
A small, sheltered beach near Lagos accessed by a wooden staircase through a cleft in tall ochre cliffs, with calm, clear Atlantic water in a rock-enclosed bowl.
Wikipedia Lagos Tourism Map
Volcanic & Dramatic ~8 entries Black, green, or red sand — lava coast, extreme colour contrast
Punalu'u Beach United States BLACK SAND
Hawaii's most accessible black-sand beach, formed from basaltic lava fragments on the Big Island's southern coast. Hawaiian green sea turtles regularly rest on the sand.
Hawaii Guide Wikipedia Map
Papakolea Beach United States GREEN SAND
One of only four green-sand beaches in the world, reached by a 5-km round-trip hike on the Big Island's Ka'ū coast. The colour comes from olivine crystals eroded from a lava cone.
Wikipedia Map
Perissa Beach Greece BLACK SAND
A long beach on Santorini's southeastern coast, backed by the volcanic mass of Mesa Vouno. Distinctive black volcanic sand with a row of beach bars and tavernas.
Wikipedia Map
Remote Access ~12 entries Boat, ferry, or long trail required — no road access
Navagio (Shipwreck Beach) Greece BOAT ONLY
A cove on Zakynthos reachable only by boat, framed by sheer white limestone cliffs and containing the rusting hull of the MV Panagiotis, stranded here in 1980.
Wikipedia Book Tour Map
Pink Sands Beach Bahamas FERRY REQUIRED
A three-kilometre beach on Harbour Island, reached by a short water-taxi crossing from North Eleuthera Airport. The pale pink hue comes from crushed coral and shell fragments.
Wikipedia Map
City & Culture ~13 entries Urban surroundings, transit access, strong local beach culture
Ipanema Brazil CITY BEACH
Rio's famous beachfront strip south of Copacabana, with the Two Brothers peaks at its far end, consistently strong surf, and a distinct neighbourhood culture around its postos.
Rio Guide Wikipedia Map
Barceloneta Spain CITY BEACH
Barcelona's central urban beach, rebuilt for the 1992 Olympics. The beach and its promenade are the city's main summer public space.
Barcelona Guide Wikipedia Map
