Best
Luxurious Hotels

Hotels defined by service, design and setting rather than nightly rate alone. What separates them from the merely expensive is almost always something that cannot be built or scaled.

Selected Hotels
01
Argentina
Alvear Palace Hotel
Recoleta · Buenos Aires
Since 1932 Art Nouveau Independent
One of South America's pre-eminent grand hotels — a seven-storey Art Nouveau building on Avenida Alvear. 189 rooms furnished with period antiques and crystal chandeliers, with an original 1930s tearoom, L'Orangerie, that has served Buenos Aires society for nine decades.
Official Site Wikipedia Map
02
Australia
Park Hyatt Sydney
Circular Quay · Sydney
From USD 900/night Opera House Views
155 rooms on the edge of Sydney Cove, built so low that every harbour-facing room sits at eye level with the Opera House shells — not above them, not below, directly across the water. The rooftop pool mirrors the same view.
Official Site Sydney Guide Map
03
United Arab Emirates
Burj Al Arab Jumeirah
Jumeirah Beach · Dubai
From USD 1,500/night 202 Duplex Suites
202 duplex suites on a man-made island connected to Jumeirah Beach by private causeway. The helipad on the 28th floor doubles as a tennis court. The underwater restaurant Scape is reached by a simulated submarine journey from the lobby. The most recognisable hotel exterior in the world.
Official Site Wikipedia Dubai Guide Map
04
United Kingdom
Claridge's
Mayfair · London
Since 1812 Art Deco Interiors
The Mayfair address that has accommodated every British monarch and most European royal families in exile. Art Deco interiors redesigned by Basil Ionides in the 1920s; 197 rooms and suites. The bar is a Michelin-starred destination in its own right.
Official Site Wikipedia London Guide Map
Design concept: Cinematic Numbered List
Replaces the showcase-grid card layout with an editorial, full-bleed alternating split. Each hotel occupies photo (½ width) + info (½ width), switching sides per entry. An oversized ghost ordinal number (200px, 16% white opacity) sits behind each photo — visible but not legible as a number, functioning as a texture that reinforces sequence and prestige. No card borders; no equal-height boxes. The scale and alternation create pacing similar to a printed hotel guide or Condé Nast Traveler spread.
