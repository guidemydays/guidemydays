More Resources

What each site is good for, and where it falls short.

The travel guides, booking tools and reference sites worth your time, each with a straight assessment of what it does well and where it stops being useful.

↓ Concept below the hero — numbered editorial reading list replacing the current four-column card grid. Each resource gets a large counter, a type tag, and an active-status badge. Hover reveals a terracotta accent bar and lifts the counter colour.
Editorial Guides
01
Fodor’s Editorial Guide Active

Curated city guides, hotel and restaurant picks with a consistent editorial standard. Good starting point for building a shortlist of neighborhoods, museums, and dining before you arrive.

02
Culture Trip Editorial Guide Active

Culturally-driven destination articles focused on arts, food, and local perspective. Laid off its editorial staff in 2023, then acquired by U.S. News & World Report in February 2024 — active again with new editorial coverage alongside the existing archive.

03
Rough Guides Editorial Guide Active

In-depth destination coverage across history, culture, and practical detail. One of the most thorough written guides for understanding a place before you arrive.

Inspiration & Hidden Places
04
Atlas Obscura Hidden Places Active

Hidden and unusual places around the world — the stops no mainstream guidebook covers. Excellent for finding something genuinely different in a city you have already been to.

05
National Geographic Inspiration Active

Destination inspiration, best-of lists, and photography-driven guides. More useful for deciding where to go than for planning once you have a destination locked in.

Practical & Regional
06
Rick Steves Europe Active

Practical Europe travel — neighborhood-level breakdowns, free audio guides, and crowd-management tips. Best for first-time visits to major European cities.

Reference & Reviews
07
Wikipedia Reference Active

Primary reference for stop history, context, and factual detail. Every stop’s Wikipedia article is the first read before building any itinerary entry.

08
TripAdvisor Reviews Active

Attraction rankings, traveler reviews, and things to do by category. Best used for cross-referencing ratings rather than as a primary discovery source.

Design concept notes

What changed: replaced the current 260px card grid (four-column, plain white cards) with a numbered editorial reading list. The counter numbers (52–64px, near-white) act as decorative anchors without overwhelming the content. A thin terracotta accent bar appears on hover via opacity transition. Category breaks become typographic chapter dividers: a small all-caps label and full-width rule instead of a section heading.

Why it reads differently: the current grid forces all cards to equal visual weight, making scanning slow. The numbered list gives each source a sequence and a natural eye-path: counter → name → tag → description. The 72ch max-width on descriptions keeps reading comfortable at all viewport widths.

What it keeps: the hero band exactly as-is, all content, all links, all status info. The palette uses only site-standard pastel and neutral tones — no vibrant or black fills. Dark-mode tokens are wired inline so the mockup reads correctly in both themes. No shared pill/badge/dropdown/search components were introduced — none are needed in this layout.
