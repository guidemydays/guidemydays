Concept notes — what changes and why

  • Immersive dark panel on result reveal. The current white .pg-card becomes a warm charcoal panel (#211e19) with a soft terracotta glow radiating from the top. The dramatic cream-to-dark shift when a country is selected is the visual "moment" — like flicking on a power switch. Hero and background stay unchanged per the _shared.md rule.
  • Editorial voltage watermark. The voltage figure (230V) renders at 130px / 5% opacity as a typographic backdrop — magazine-style depth. It does not compete with readable content; it anchors the card's electrical identity without text.
  • Answer line is the billboard. "Bring a Type C, F or L adapter" is pushed to 26px bold — the loudest readable element after the country name. Type letters stay terracotta for instant scannability. A reader on an airport shopping floor can identify their adapter type in one look.
  • 3-column plug grid on desktop / 1-column horizontal on mobile. Each plug type gets its own named card (badge + name + note + image pair), replacing the current stacked full-width cards. On mobile, cards flip horizontal: meta left, image pair right — compact but readable at 14px tap targets.
  • Plug type badge. A terracotta-tinted pill on each card names the type letter prominently. Currently the type letter only appears in the card title — the badge makes it instantly scannable when multiple types are shown.
  • Images in production. The 🔌/🔋 placeholders in this mockup are replaced by the real plugs/imgs/type-{letter}-*.jpg photos, displayed in a dark-surface frame (80×80px) that makes the photo subject pop against the dark background — contrast is higher than the current white-on-white card.
  • Research source. Inspired by interactive tool designs on Awwwards (dark-reveal, ambient glow), voltage checker sites using editorial type-as-watermark, and airline booking UI patterns where the "answer" state is visually dominant vs the "searching" state.
