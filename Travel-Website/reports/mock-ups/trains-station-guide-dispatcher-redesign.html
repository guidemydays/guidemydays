🇫🇷 Gare du Nord
Paris, France

Critical
Platforms aren't posted until 15–20 minutes before departure. The entire departure hall moves at once when the board updates — go when it shows, not before.

Eurostar runs from its own international terminal — separate security screening, passport control, and departure lounge. Find that entrance before any queue.

2026's EU Entry/Exit System adds a fingerprint-and-photo kiosk step for first non-EU/UK crossings. Arrive on the early end of the check-in window, not the minimum.

🇯🇵 Shinjuku Station
Tokyo, Japan

Critical
Switching operators (JR East → Odakyu → Tokyo Metro) means exiting one company's ticket gates and re-entering another's — not just walking to a different platform.

Note the exit number printed near your ticket gate before going underground. Retracing without it loses the better part of an hour — East, West, South and New South exits surface blocks apart.

IC card top-ups inside one company's gates don't help once you've crossed to another's. Build extra minutes into any transfer.

Critical
The Metro entrance isn't visible from the main departure hall — it's down a separate staircase off the underground concourse. The departure boards won't point you to it.

The upper Galleria level (shops and food) sits a full floor above platforms — track access is always one level down.

Trenitalia / RFI staff wear branded uniforms. Unsolicited help with bags at platform level is worth waving off without engaging.

Critical
The track (Gleis) number tells you the level — 1 to 8 are underground, 11 to 16 are up top. Read it first; it stops a wrong-level dash to the escalators.

The glass design lets you see across the whole building, which is deceptive — the two rail lines cross at right angles on different levels. Close by sight can still be two escalators away.

Check the Wagenstandsanzeiger board for your carriage sector (A–G), not just the platform number, especially with a reserved seat.

🇬🇧 King's Cross / St Pancras
London, United Kingdom
Guide
2

~250 m
Apart — about 3–4 min walk
St Pancras
Only — Eurostar never departs from King's Cross
Critical
King's Cross and St Pancras are two different stations — not two entrances to one building. Eurostar departs only from St Pancras International. Showing up at the wrong one is a repeated, real mistake.

The two share an Underground station wedged between them — which is exactly why people conflate them. The walk between street-level entrances is about 3–4 minutes.

Arriving on a domestic train into King's Cross to catch a Eurostar from St Pancras is not "already at the station" — budget the walk as its own step.
